# International Review — Finland (esiopetus / grades 1–2 lens)

**Reviewer stance:** Finnish early-education specialist (esiopetus and grades 1–2, University of Helsinki teacher-education tradition), reviewing a home learning system built for a culturally close Estonian family. My lens is the Finnish one: play-first kindergarten, formal academics at 7, phenomenon-based integration, minimal homework, the primacy of rest / outdoor life / well-being, trust in the child, and deep suspicion of early testing and pressure. Finland and Estonia share the slow clock and the transparent orthography; where this review says "we," it means that shared Nordic-Baltic position, and where Finland and this system genuinely differ by *choice*, I say so rather than pretending evidence settles it.

**Documents read in full:** `curriculum/learning-path.md` v1.6 (including the JSON block), `docs/methodology/combined-framework.md` v1.6. For grounding: `docs/research/01–04`, `README.md`, `STATE.md`, the 1a/1b booklet verification. **Read first, as instructed:** `gap-review-1-practitioner.md`, `gap-review-2-systems.md`, `gap-fix-verification.md` — everything those reviews raised and v1.6 fixed (CR17 scope division, CR18 read-aloud rail, the life stratum, `sci-body-health`, `lit-reads-for-fun`, `dig-media-sense`, `num-chance`, B22/SSP delivery, re-entry protocol, refer-out triggers, check administration and language rulings, Archetype-B guard-rails, tier-4 trigger, minimum-viable week, multi-child ruling, autonomy-inside-dose ruling) is treated as **settled and not re-raised**. Where a finding below touches a standing ruling (CR12, CR14), it challenges the ruling's *warrant or rendering*, which is an external reviewer's job, not a re-litigation of an already-fixed gap.

**Surface tags:** every finding is marked **[CURRICULUM]** (the two governing documents and graph) or **[APP]** (the planned family progress app — specifically the proposed mastered=green / learning=yellow / not-learned=red marking, which appears in no shipped document and is reviewed here as a design proposal).

---

## Overall verdict

This is the most honestly self-policed home curriculum I have reviewed — its anti-pressure law (no streaks, no red ink, no comparisons, no deadlines, failure as "not yet") is Finnish in spirit and better *written down* than most Finnish schools manage — and yet it remains, at heart, an achievement architecture wearing play's clothing: the kindergarten band is guarded by register rulings while the measurement machinery underneath (roughly fifteen Archetype-A check events at tier 0, each run twice, plus strip-borne retests, administered since v1.6 under a deliberately neutral-faced protocol with no age scoping) is something no Finnish educator would run on a five-year-old, and the system's second scheduled run-ahead — numeracy on the "international consensus clock" — is a family preference presented as evidence when the project's own research shows the slow-clock countries winning. The proposed family-app traffic light (green/yellow/**red**) is the one element I would refuse outright: red-marking unlearned skills to a 5–9-year-old violates the framework's own house law (GP9, CR11, §5.1, §5.5) before it violates mine, and would quietly convert a wonder-map into a deficit report. Everything else is fixable with scoped rulings, one seasonal project rhythm, and a palette that only ever *adds* color.

**Finding counts: 1 blocking · 9 important · 6 nice-to-have.**

## 1. Is the age-5–6 tier genuinely play-first?

**What I verified and credit before criticizing** — because a hard review must be a fair one:

- The register law is real and specific: GP11 (play register at 5–6), CR2 (available early, demanded never), CR3 (the dose is ≤10 min inside a 10–15-min session, on ~2 of 6 weekly sessions only at Band 1), B2 (zero-reading icon instructions), TR4 skins on tier-0 titles.
- The motivational bans are the strongest I have seen in a commercial or home product: no streaks, no per-page tokens, no leaderboards, no sibling comparison, no red ink, no test aesthetics (CR4, CR11, §5.5, B20). CR4's reasoning — "a family trip must never feel like losing" — could be lifted from a Finnish ECEC guidance document.
- Boss gates carry no deadlines; mastery-not-age is enforced, not aspirational; the tier-0 Mastered-satisfies exception kills the hidden consolidation tax; the frontier is child-chosen from 3–5 surfaced nodes.
- The 1b unlock trial as shipped (two fresh puzzle grids ≥3 days apart, solved alone, self-checked, no scores, no timer — verified conformant by the gap-fix report) shows the system *can* run a check that is indistinguishable from play. This matters for FIN-6 below: the standard exists in-house.

So: does the mastery machinery leak achievement pressure into kindergarten? **Structurally, no — the bans hold. Registrally, partly — the check apparatus does. And the planned app would reopen every door the documents closed** (Section 5).

### FIN-6 — [CURRICULUM] IMPORTANT — The check-administration protocol is unscoped by age, and tier-0 Archetype-A checks are table-sittings in a band Finland assesses only by observation.

- **Evidence.** Finnish esiopetus (age 6, R3 §2.2) carries *no* reading or arithmetic outcome requirements and its assessment is entirely descriptive-observational; grades 1–2 assessment is verbal/formative with no standardized testing. This system's tier 0 (age 5–6) carries ~15 Archetype-A performance checks (counting, subitising, bonds-of-5, phonemic play, letter knowledge, retell, sorting, sudoku, robot game, observation drawing, idea fluency…), each "5–10 items, <10 minutes, passed twice ≥3 days apart" (§5.2), each later re-verified via strip-borne retest (§5.1). That is on the order of thirty-plus formal sittings across the kindergarten band, before the koolieelik year begins.
- **The v1.6 aggravation.** The new check-administration protocol (§5.2) — neutral face and voice, no hints, no mid-check teaching, first-error-is-information, stop after two misses — was added for validity, and for a 7–9-year-old it is right. But it carries **no age scoping**, and at 5 a silent, neutral-faced, deliberately non-helping parent *is* test aesthetics in the child's phenomenology, whatever CR11 says the frame is. A five-year-old reads the parent's face, finds it professionally blank, and learns: *this moment is different; I am being measured.* CR11's ban on "mimicking school testing" is violated socially while being honored materially.
- **What Finland would sign instead.** At Band 1, checks should be **observed, not administered**: the parent notices, in ordinary play across ordinary days, that the child counts 17 buttons with cardinality, and logs it — exactly how the system already treats Archetype-B nodes, and exactly how esiopetus certifies koolivalmidus-relevant skill. Most tier-0 A-checks are visible in normal play or in the booklet pages themselves (the 1b trial proves the format).
- **Fix (small, scoped).** One §5.2 ruling: *(a)* at Band 1, Archetype-A checks may be evidenced by accumulated observation of the criterion behavior in play (logged like B-instances), or run as a game the child does not experience as a sitting — the 1b trial is the named template; *(b)* the neutral-face administration protocol applies from Band 2 (its validity rationale is real, and a 6–7-year-old entering formal strands can meet it); *(c)* Band-1 retrieval strips are explicitly game-format (icon-and-tile, B2), stated once so booklet authors cannot drift. Nothing about the criteria changes — only the register of verification, which is precisely the thing GP11 says the band is about.

### FIN-7 — [CURRICULUM] NICE-TO-HAVE — Career wonders are named and visible from day 1; Finland would grade the rendering by era.

CR12 is a genuinely good ruling (wonders not gates, quests at 8+, tenth design-your-own slot, and gap review 2's P8 fix making the valve demonstrably real — all settled, not re-raised). The residual Finnish objection is to the *tier-0 poster* specifically: a five-year-old does not need nine named professions on the horizon of their play map, and R1 TR7 — the project's own source — says "no career-specific pressure below ~8." Visible-but-greyed is defensible Civ lore; visible-named-with-path-highlighting-on-request at 5 invites the instrumental question ("what is this FOR?") three years before the design wants to answer it. **Fix:** in the poster spec (unbuilt, so this costs nothing now): the Spark-era poster renders the wonders as an unnamed decorative horizon (a city skyline, a distant mountain range); names and door-cards appear from the Builder/Explorer posters, matching when quests actually unlock. Path-highlighting stays an *answer to the child's question*, never ambient display, at every age — which §1 of the learning path already half-says.

## 2. Dose and balance — desk minutes, homework culture, rest

The framework's negative-space protection ("everything outside the session is ordinary childhood," binding session caps, the CR18 rail as the sole daily item, minimum-viable-week rule, yields-to-school) is stronger than anything I have seen written for a family product, and I verified the arithmetic: the templates and §5.6 prices are internally consistent. Two things are still wrong, and both are about what the budget *omits*, not what it contains.

### FIN-8 — [CURRICULUM] IMPORTANT — §5.6 prices the system's minutes and never the child's whole day: lasteaed and school (with its homework) are invisible in the load table, so the *combined* desk load at Bands 2–4 is unbudgeted.

- **Evidence.** §5.6 prices child system-minutes (Band 2: 90–120/wk; Band 3: 75–100/wk; Band 4: ~100/wk) plus the rail (~70/wk). But the Band-2 child also attends a full lasteaed koolieelik day (which itself delivers koolivalmidus prep, per CR17's own division of labor), and the Band-3/4 child attends Estonian school *and carries Estonian school homework* — which, unlike Finland's near-zero norm at 7–8, is real from grade 1 in most Estonian schools. The only acknowledgment is the crunch-week yield rule — an exception, not a budget.
- **The Finnish norm for comparison.** A Finnish 7–8-year-old's total out-of-school desk time is commonly 0–20 minutes a day. This system's *design* week at Band 3 is ~15–20 min/day of system work plus school homework on top — plausibly 25–40 min/day combined, roughly double the Finnish child's total, at the age band where Finland's results say less suffices. I am not claiming the system alone is heavy (it is not); I am claiming **nobody is watching the sum**, and the sum is the child's actual day.
- **Fix.** Add a school-side row to the §5.6 table (an honest estimate of lasteaed/school + homework hours per band, marked "not ours, but on the same child"); rule a **combined daily desk cap** for Bands 3–4 (e.g., system + school homework together target ≤30–40 min/day, with the system auto-shedding first — practice pages before strips, per the existing minimum-viable-week ordering); and promote the crunch-week yield from exception to standing budget: on any day school homework exceeds its estimate, the system session is the thing that shrinks. This is one paragraph, and it converts "the system yields to school" from a sentence into a mechanism — the same move CR18 and the re-entry protocol already made for their values.

### FIN-9 — [CURRICULUM] IMPORTANT — The numeracy run-ahead (CR14's second) is a family preference wearing an evidence costume; Finland would call the Band-2 scheduled number dose push, not pull.

- **Evidence.** CR14 names two scheduled run-aheads and calls both "evidence-endorsed." For English (GP10) the necessity argument is real: opaque orthography, 2–3-year runway, no school delivery — I accept it (see FIN-15 for the honesty residue). For numeracy the cited endorsement is R3 §6's Implications recommending the UK/Singapore number clock — a recommendation sitting directly beside R3's own headline finding #2: *the late-starting systems (Estonia, Finland, Singapore's play-based K) outperform the early-starting ones by ages 10 and 15*, with Estonia Europe's top PISA performer **on the slower clock**. Duncan 2007 (early math predicts later achievement) is correlational school-readiness evidence, not evidence that scheduling a to-be-schooled child a year ahead adds anything. The mastery-gating, no-deadline, "available not demanded" protections are real and I verified them — but the **weekly template is not pull**: 2 of 6 koolieelik-year sessions are *number-led dose sessions by schedule* (§3), targeting within-20 fluency machinery at an age where koolivalmidus asks for ±5 and Finland asks for nothing. Content availability is pull; scheduled dose slots are push. CR2's compromise ("available early, demanded never") is honored for content and quietly broken for time.
- **What this costs.** Two of six weekly kindergarten sessions, for years, buying arrival at the Estonian grade-3 list one year early — a destination the child reaches anyway, in a school system that is already Europe's best at teaching it. Double-teaching (home now, school next year) is the classic inefficiency our systems were built to avoid.
- **Fix (two honest options, either acceptable).** *(a)* Reword CR14: the numeracy run-ahead is "evidence-permitted and family-chosen," not evidence-endorsed — one sentence, the house style of every v1.6 overclaim repair; **and** make the Band-2 number dose conditional on child pull (if the child is not pulling, the two number-led slots revert to booklet/free-choice — the frontier still surfaces number roots, Montessori-style). Or *(b)* keep the schedule and add the honesty sentence plus a named review point (if the child shows no pull by mid-Band-2, de-schedule). What may not stand is the current framing, in which a preference cites a consensus clock while the project's own evidence table shows the slow clock winning.

### FIN-10 — [CURRICULUM] NICE-TO-HAVE — Outdoor daily life is protected only as residue; name it as soil, the way CR18 named the read-aloud.

The framework protects free play negatively (session caps bound the system; everything else is childhood) — genuinely well. But Finland would notice that the one daily rhythm our tradition holds sacred at 5–9 — outdoor time, in all weather, every day — appears nowhere, while the one daily rhythm the *system* needed (the read-aloud rail) got a named, priced, protected ruling. The asymmetry teaches a quiet lesson about what the family values. **Fix:** one sentence in §3 or §5.6, CR18's pattern without CR18's machinery: the daily outdoor hour is named as the soil the system grows in — never scheduled, never logged, never a check — and the seasonal layer already gives it hooks (the folk calendar lives outdoors). Zero cost; it makes the value visible.

## 3. What Finland would call missing

(All checked against CR17's scope rulings first: music, art, PE/swimming, civics are ruled school-delivered — those are settled and none of the findings below re-opens them.)

### FIN-11 — [CURRICULUM] IMPORTANT — No phenomenon-based, cross-domain project exists before the tier-3 quests; integration below age 8 lives entirely inside single-domain columns.

- **Evidence.** Finland requires at least one multidisciplinary phenomenon module per year for every basic-education pupil, and esiopetus is *organized* as five integrative entities rather than subjects (R3 §2.2). In this system, the first genuinely cross-domain, sustained, meaning-shaped unit a child meets is their tier-3 career quest at ~8 — and the quests are good phenomenon work, I credit that (teddy clinic, restaurant night, and family show are integrative projects any Finnish teacher would recognize). But for three years before that, the child's system-life is domain columns: B13's Life/Beauty/Knowledge rotation integrates *within* a mechanic, `ef-multistep-projects` is generic project skill, the seasonal layer is a scheduler, and CR6's provocation page is a single page. Nothing before tier 3 starts from a **child's question about the world** and pulls three branches through it — which is the actual Finnish (and Reggio, per the project's own R1) definition of integration, and the thing a phenomenon module exists to teach: that the world is not divided into columns.
- **Fix (rides existing machinery, no new nodes).** A **seasonal phenomenon week** ruled into §3's seasonal layer: 2–4 times a year, the two booklet/free-choice sessions of one week are given to one child-chosen question (*Why is it dark so long? Where does bread come from? What happens to our trash?*), pulled across ≥3 domains, documented on B19 surfaces, wild-node-compatible (a phenomenon that catches fire becomes a claimed wild node — see FIN-12). One §3 paragraph plus one booklet-pipeline idiom. This also gives the folk-calendar layer (CR17) its natural vehicle: mardipäev *is* a phenomenon week waiting to be run.

### FIN-12 — [CURRICULUM] IMPORTANT — Child agency over *content* is deferred entirely to an unbuilt poster spec, and the wild-node valve never connects to the frontier mechanism that actually governs the child's week.

- **Evidence.** The child's ruled agency is: choose among 3–5 *parent-curated, engineered-graph* frontier nodes (§5.1), choose order/format within sessions (the settled P9 fix). Content agency — the Finnish non-negotiable that the child's own interests steer real learning time — lives in wild nodes (CR6, ~10–15% of territory) and the design-your-own slot: both poster-spec constructs, both unbuilt, and **neither mentioned by the frontier rule**. As specified today, a wild node can exist on the poster while never being one of the 3–5 surfaced choices — the two systems are never wired together, so curation can (and under schedule pressure, will) squeeze emergent interest to zero while every ruling stays technically satisfied. This is distinct from gap review 2's P8 (make the tenth slot first-class — settled): P8 made the valve *real*; this finding makes it *reachable*.
- **Fix.** Two sentences in §5.1: once a child has claimed a wild node, **one surfaced frontier slot always carries it** (or another child-claimed item) until the child retires it — the ~10–15% territory share becomes a floor with a delivery mechanism, not a ceiling with a hope. And the starting-frontier rule's "one the child picks from the rest" extends to "or anything the child proposes" — the Reggio clause, one clause long.

### FIN-13 — [CURRICULUM] IMPORTANT — Sustainability and ecological responsibility are absent from a tree whose horizon is nine careers, ventures, and money.

- **Evidence.** Sustainable lifestyle is woven through the Finnish ECEC/basic curricula as a value and competence; Estonia's own national curriculum carries *keskkond ja jätkusuutlik areng* as a cross-cutting theme (läbiv teema) — outside R3's milestone matrices, i.e., the same upstream narrowing CR17 already named and partially repaired. The v1.6 additions cover the *relationship* seeds honestly (nature observation, seasons, the care-for-a-living-thing log in `sci-body-health` — credited, settled). What exists nowhere is the **responsibility** strand: the tree teaches profit = sell − cost at 8 (`quest-entrepreneur`, `t4-venture-lab`) and never materials, waste, repair, or enough-ness; `cre-iterate-v2` iterates designs without ever asking what version 2 *consumes*; no Life page sorts waste; no exercise repairs instead of replacing. For a system this explicit about what its wonder-set teaches ("worthy futures cluster here," §6's own words), the silence is a values statement: the economic loop is taught with its natural-resource half missing.
- **Fix (costume-level, no new nodes — the system's own cheapest idiom).** Name sustainability in §6 as a cross-cutting lens (one row-note); add costume lines: a waste-sort/repair Life page in the B13 rotation; "version 2 uses less" as a named `cre-iterate-v2` / `t4-design-build` variant; honest materials-cost (and leftovers) accounting in `quest-entrepreneur` and `t4-venture-lab`'s P&L idiom; a give-back exercise in the SCI chain (the child's care-log plant idiom extended outdoors). One editing pass.

### FIN-14 — [CURRICULUM] NICE-TO-HAVE — Multiliteracy exists in pieces and is named nowhere.

Finland's L4 (interpret and produce meaning across text types: image, chart, gesture, media) is genuinely half-present: `dig-media-sense` (settled) carries the critical-reading half; charts live in NUM/SCI; the persuasion poster, family-show storyboard, and B19 documentation carry production. But no document names the lens, so booklet authors will not thread it deliberately — and the framework's own GP14 (many costumes) is the natural carrier. **Fix:** one §6 structural note naming multiliteracy as a cross-domain lens with its existing carriers listed; booklet pipeline told to pair interpret/produce where cheap (read a chart → make a chart; watch an ad → make a "sell your invention" poster). No graph change.

## 4. What Finland would say is overbuilt

**What is *not* overbuilt, checked and said plainly:** the LOG/sudoku line (play-shaped, self-checking, strategy withheld until S5 — Finland would happily use these booklets); the SCI observation trunk (habits before content is our own doctrine); the SEL/EF strand (observation-tallied, never drilled); the life stratum; the read-aloud rail; the Estonian-literacy support-don't-duplicate stance (KÄTS logic, correctly borrowed). The system's play-shaped majority is real.

### FIN-15 — [CURRICULUM] NICE-TO-HAVE — The English SSP apparatus never names the lighter Nordic path it rejected; one opportunity-cost sentence would complete the house honesty.

Finland produces some of Europe's best English speakers with **no English synthetic phonics at all** — oral-first school English from ~7–9, subtitled media, exposure — reaching by ~12–13 the endpoint this system engineers for 9. The system's by-9 goal is the family's right, GP10's necessity argument (school won't supply it; opaque orthography needs a program *if you want it early*) is internally sound, and B22/§5.4's delivery machinery is the correct consequence of the choice. But the framework argues dose adequacy in detail while never stating the alternative: *the Nordic exposure path exists, costs ~zero desk minutes, and reaches the same place ~3–4 years later.* A doctrine this scrupulous about naming rejected options (every CR does it) should name this one — because it is the single largest recurring spend in the child's week (2 of 6 sessions for ~3 years), and a future tired-family decision point ("do we drop phonics this winter?") deserves the honest trade-off in writing. **Fix:** one sentence in GP10 or CR2. No mechanical change.

### FIN-16 — [CURRICULUM] NICE-TO-HAVE — The boss checks' numeric latency bars institutionalize covert timing; Finland would certify fluency by listening alone.

`num-addsub-20` and `num-times-tables` specify "9/10 at roughly 3 seconds per fact... no visible stopwatch, no racing." A parent cannot apply a 3-second criterion without timing; a hidden stopwatch is still a stopwatch, and children detect covert evaluation faster than overt (the parent glancing at a watch mid-check *is* the test aesthetic CR11 bans). The specs already contain the better criterion in the same sentence: *the parent listens for retrieval or a fast derived fact, not finger-counting.* That qualitative listen is how a Finnish grade-2 teacher certifies exactly this, and it is sufficient — R2's automaticity construct is about strategy, not milliseconds. **Fix:** drop the numeric bar from the parent-facing spec ("answers promptly, without counting — listen for retrieval" carries the whole load); keep the 9/10 and two-sittings structure. Two node cards, one framework line.

*(Also weighed and left alone: the two-stage consolidation machinery — ~4 check events per node — is more measurement than any Nordic classroom runs, but it is invisible to the child by design and priced by rule; it stays defensible exactly as long as it stays invisible, which is the app's problem — Section 5. The handwriting copy-passage duplicates school drill but already rides warm-up lanes and sheds first in thin weeks; not worth a finding.)*

## 5. The progress app — the red/yellow/green question

The planned family app proposes marking skills **mastered = green / learning = yellow / not-learned = red**. This section answers the direct question asked of me: *is red-marking skills developmentally sound for a 5–9-year-old to see about themselves?* **No. I would not sign it, no Finnish colleague would sign it, and — decisive even if you dismiss the Finns — the system's own governing framework already bans it four separate ways.**

### FIN-1 — [APP] BLOCKING — The red/yellow/red traffic light converts the wonder-map into a deficit report; "red = not learned" must not exist on any child-visible surface.

- **The developmental case.** A 5–9-year-old reads red normatively — school socialization makes red mean *wrong, bad, stop* years before it means *not yet scheduled*. The trichotomy collapses §5.1's carefully built six-state machine into a deficit frame: **Locked** (content the child was never meant to meet yet — sealed scrolls by CR13) becomes indistinguishable from *failed*. And the arithmetic is merciless: a 6-year-old stands before a 108-node tree holding perhaps 15–20 green cells — the app would show the child a self-portrait that is ~85% red **for years**, by design, with the red receding only as childhood passes. GP9 cites Stipek's optimism-protection precisely against making normative standing salient at this age; R2 §9.4 places the motivational fragility window at 8–10 — the exact ages the app would serve.
- **The system's own law, violated four ways.** (1) §5.5/GP9: "no red ink" — literal. (2) CR11: no test aesthetics — a traffic-light dashboard is the aesthetic of a school gradebook. (3) §5.1: decay "dims the poster, never re-locks it... a summer holiday may dim the map, it must not darken it" — under R/Y/G, where does Needs-polish go? Any answer (green→yellow demotion, or worse, red) renders regression as visible loss, the exact experience CR4 banned when it banned streaks. (4) §5.4's atypical-development ruling softens dimming because "the dimming poster is a demoralization engine for exactly the ADHD profile" — a red state is that engine with the safety removed.
- **What to build instead (the poster already solved this).** Color is only ever **added**, never turned against the child: *unrevealed* (neutral parchment — sealed scrolls, not failure), *available* (a glow/outline — invitation), *practicing* (partial fill / pencil ticks — the cluster idiom), *mastered/consolidated* (full color + stamp — the child's own coloring act), *needs-polish* (slight fade with a small "let's polish" icon, appearing only after the quarterly dim-check, never live — see FIN-5). The child's map fills in like a coloring book. This is not a compromise palette; it is a *strictly more informative* one (five honest states instead of three false ones).
- **The parent-only view.** Even granting a parent-gated screen, "red = not learned" misframes Locked as deficit for the parent too — and parent framing becomes dinner-table language. Parent views should speak §5.1's state names. If a triage color is wanted, red may mark **system obligations** (an overdue retest queue, a decomposition trigger firing) — never a child's skill. And a family app must be *assumed child-visible*: phones are shared, shoulders are looked over. The blocking severity attaches to the design, not to a display flag.

### FIN-2 — [APP] IMPORTANT — CR13 (curated frontier, one era, sealed scrolls) binds the poster and does not yet bind the app; extend it by name before any screen ships.

The documents rule the child's view precisely: one era poster (~20–40 nodes), a 3–5-node frontier, future tiers as sealed scrolls — because Khan's free-roam map demonstrably caused paralysis and grinding (CR13's own evidence). An app's natural affordances — scroll the whole tree, expand everything, update instantly — are that anti-pattern with better production values. No current ruling stops it, because the rulings were written for paper. **Fix:** one framework sentence extending CR13 and CR12's display rules to *any digital family surface*: era-scoped default view, frontier-first navigation, tiers beyond current+1 rendered as sealed scrolls, careers rendered as wonders (never as progress bars), and the internal DAG never shown to the child (§1's "the child never sees this file" applies to screens too).

### FIN-3 — [APP] IMPORTANT — Rule out the aggregate metrics and attention mechanics *now*: no percentages, no counts, no notifications, no timers, no cross-child dashboards.

Specific bans the documents imply but nowhere state for an app, each one Finnish assessment culture would insist on: **(a)** no "X/108 mastered," no per-era completion percent, no "% toward Doctor" — a fraction toward a career is exactly the instrumental framing CR12 exists to prevent, and a completion metric turns the tree into a grind (R4's own anti-pattern list); **(b)** no push notifications or reminders — a reminder is a streak engine in disguise, and CR4 banned the mechanism, not just the pixel; the *parent's* quarterly dim-check is the only sanctioned cadence; **(c)** no visible session timers (CR16's dose/ceiling logic is the parent's, not the child's); **(d)** one profile per child with no shared comparative screen — §5.6's multi-child ruling (one poster per child, siblings never benchmarks) extends to information architecture, where a "family overview" page would rebuild the comparison the wall-poster rule was written to prevent. **Fix:** a short "app charter" appendix in the framework (ten lines) so the app inherits the doctrine instead of accidentally re-litigating it.

### FIN-4 — [APP] IMPORTANT — The child owns the record (GP13); an auto-marking app takes the marking out of the child's hands.

GP13's whole architecture — the child colors the node, the parent signs, evidence attaches, "assessment and motivation are the same object" — is Reggio/Finnish documentation pedagogy: the *act of recording* is the pedagogy, not the data. An app that flips states from a parent's tap silently reassigns authorship of the record to the adult and the machine. **Fix:** rule the app a **mirror of the paper poster, never the source of truth for the child**: mastery is celebrated at the wall (child colors, parent signs — the existing ceremony), and the app records what the wall already shows; child-facing celebration stays physical. The app's real value-add is the parent-side ops the documents already assign to tooling (retest queues, dim-check sweeps, placement sweeps, decomposition triggers, evidence-log lines) — build that half first; it is the half with no developmental risk.

### FIN-5 — [APP] NICE-TO-HAVE — Decay must not tick live on a screen.

§5.6 deliberately makes decay *quarterly and batched* (the ~20-minute dim-check sweep) — the poster dims four times a year at most, and never during a declared pause (re-entry protocol). A synced app that recomputes decay timers daily would surface slow fading as continuous ambient loss — un-Finnish and un-CR4 — and would bypass the declared-pause suspension. **Fix:** the app applies Needs-polish rendering only when the parent runs the quarterly sweep (a button, not a daemon), honors declared pauses, and implements the §5.4 softened-dim rendering for documented difficulties as the default code path, not an option.

## 6. Checked and NOT findings (so the next reviewer need not re-check)

- **Play-first structure at Band 1** — sessions child-chosen, dose ≤10 min on 2 of 6 sessions, booklet spreads unfixed: genuinely play-weighted; my FIN-6 concerns verification register only.
