## Overall verdict

This is the first Western home system I have reviewed that takes mastery machinery as seriously as we do — the consolidation state machine, the strip-borne retest arithmetic, the recognition-before-production battery and the honest dose accounting are of a standard I would show to trainee teachers. But it has learned Singapore's *structure* and not yet Singapore's *craft*: the §6 numeracy row claims "Singapore CPA spine" while the model method exists as a single tier-3 debut with no pictorial ramp, one-step word problems and comparison problems ("how many more" — the problem type the project's own R2 calls hardest) have no home anywhere in the graph, variation theory never landed as a booklet rule, there is no non-routine problem culture, and the difficulty system has a ruled floor response (GP17) with no ceiling response — the able child's stretch is left to acceleration, which is the one lever Singapore reaches for last. The bilingual strand is better designed than most of what I see at home in Singapore — the division of labor with the societal language is exactly the right inversion of our problem — but the CR18 rail prices parent minutes without ruling the English share, and productive spoken English is deferred wholesale to tier 4 when a costless invited-production seed belongs at 6–8. None of this breaks the graph; almost all of it is sub-checks, exercise lines and one or two B-rules. The one blocking item sits ahead of the documents: the progress app as currently conceived (green/yellow/red) contradicts the framework's own display law and must be specified against §5.1/§5.5/CR13 before it ships.

**Finding counts: 1 blocking · 8 important · 3 nice-to-have.**

## 1. Mathematical depth — does the spine reach Singapore-grade depth by 9?

**Mostly yes on content range, and genuinely yes on CPA — said first, because it is earned.** Verified per node, not per citation: bonds-of-5 uses part-whole ladybirds and hidden-finger games before symbols; bonds-to-10 uses ten-frames with missing-part formats and equals-as-balance from the start (McNeil honored); teens are built as ten-and-ones with materials; within-20 crosses ten via make-ten on ten-frames; within-100 draws tens-and-ones pictures before column forms; multiplication runs equal-groups/arrays a full tier before any retrieval demand (concept-before-drill, the order R3 credits us with); tables stage 2·5·10·3·4 → 6·7·8·9, which is *my* P2→P3 staging exactly; fractions go fair shares → number line → first equivalence. The two boss milestones sit precisely on the five-system consensus rows. The endpoint (10,000, full tables with division facts, fractions 1/2–1/5, two-step problems) is Estonian-grade-3-anchored and lands within a whisker of P3. This is not CPA-as-citation; it is CPA per node.

**Where it is not Singapore-grade is the problem-solving layer — the part we consider the point of arithmetic.**

### S-1. One-step word problems and comparison problems have no home; the model method debuts unstaged — IMPORTANT [CURRICULUM DOCS]
- **Evidence.** "Word problem" appears in exactly one node in the whole graph: `num-problems-data` (tier 3, 8–9) — "solves two-step word problems by drawing bar models first." Below it: `num-addsub-10` has "bond-and-story pages" (a story *context*, not a problem *type*), `num-addsub-20`'s exercises are make-ten bridges and balance formats, `num-addsub-100`'s are pictures and number-line hops, `num-money-time`'s are shop pages. No node anywhere carries a can-do for solving one-step word problems across the four structures (join, separate, part-whole, compare), which CCSS G1, Singapore P1 and Estonian grade-1 practice all expect from ~6–7. Worse: **comparison problems ("how many more?") appear at arithmetic level nowhere** — `num-subitise-compare` does more/fewer on sets at 5, and then the difference structure vanishes — although the project's own R2 §11 explicitly mandates "include zero and 'how many more' comparison problems, which children find hardest," and the comparison bar model exists in my tradition precisely because this type defeats children who can compute. And "bar model" has zero matches in the framework doctrine: the model method is not a B-rule, not a house representation, not a ramp — it is one exercise line at tier 3.
- **The node-grain incoherence.** As written, `num-problems-data` introduces the bar-model representation *and* two-step problem depth *simultaneously* — the exact stacking of two new difficulties that B7 forbids at page level and GP6 forbids in principle. My P2 children meet the part-whole model on **one-step** problems they can already solve numerically (the model earns trust on easy ground), then comparison models, then two-step composition in P3. The tree's only model node makes the representation and the depth arrive together, on the hardest content.
- **Where Singapore would insert model drawing (the staged ramp, all as sub-checks/exercise lines — no new nodes needed):**
  1. **`num-addsub-20` (tier 1):** one-step change and part-whole word problems within 20, solved orally/with materials, with the first *pictorial* part-whole bar drawn beside the ten-frame — the bar as "the ladybird picture grown up." (The number-bond diagram already in the tree from `num-bonds-5` is the concrete stage; this is its pictorial continuation — the CPA logic the system itself preaches.)
  2. **`num-addsub-100` (tier 2):** the **comparison model** — "Liis has 34, Mart has 8 more; how many has Mart? how many together?" — with "how many more/fewer" named as a sub-check, closing the R2 r22 mandate.
  3. **`num-mult-groups` (tier 2):** the equal-groups bar beside the array — one representation, two costumes (GP14 does the work here).
  4. **`num-problems-data` (tier 3):** then adds only what is genuinely new — *composing two steps* in one model — and its existing "draw the model before writing any number sentence" line becomes honest, because the model was taught before it was demanded.
- **A trunk question to consider (sub-point, nice-to-have grade).** `common_trunk`'s numeracy leg ends at `num-times-tables`; `num-problems-data` — bar-model word problems, *Estonian grade-3 checkpoint content, the family's legal reference point* — is outside the trunk, so lawyer-, entrepreneur- and youtuber-pulled children reach it only by curation, not by any displayed path or hard edge. If the checkpoint is the external anchor, the trunk arguably should cap at checkpoint content, not at the boss gate (a two-id, ancestor-closed extension: `num-multidigit`, `num-problems-data`). Flagged for consideration, not demanded — the schedule may legitimately carry it, but then the schedule should say so.
- **Fix scope:** sub-checks/exercise lines on four existing nodes + one framework sentence adding the model method to the NUM row's stated spine (after which §6's "Singapore CPA spine" claim — currently true for CPA, half-true for the model method — becomes fully true; the honest-rewording tradition of v1.6 applies).

### S-2. No problem-solving heuristics repertoire, no non-routine problem culture — IMPORTANT [CURRICULUM DOCS, lands in the booklet pipeline]
- **Evidence.** My framework's pentagon puts *processes* — heuristics, thinking skills — beside concepts and skills. R3 §2.5 names it. In the tree, the heuristics repertoire is one-tenth implemented: the sudoku line trains systematic search and elimination superbly (credited below), and nothing anywhere trains draw-a-picture-to-solve, guess-and-check, make-a-systematic-list, or work-backwards *on numbers*. Every numeracy exercise line in tiers 0–3 is skill-practice-shaped: the method is always given. A Singapore workbook's back section — problems where the child must *choose* the attack — has no equivalent in the B-rules, and "non-routine" appears in no document. This matters beyond mathematics: three careers' §2 profiles (software, finance, scientist) claim exactly this disposition.
- **Fix:** a small B-rule or a §4.4-style note: every numeracy booklet from tier 2 carries one "puzzler page" of 2–3 non-routine problems (method not given, more than one legitimate attack, parent footer says "slower is the point" — B17 already owns that sentence), with the heuristics named-as-met over time the way sudoku strategies are (self-found first, named later — the S5 idiom, which is exactly right and already in the house style). Ties into S-6's stretch mechanism.

### S-3. Depth niggles at the endpoint — NICE-TO-HAVE [CURRICULUM DOCS]
Three one-line repairs, none worth a node: (a) **division with remainder** appears nowhere, though R3 §3.2's own age-9 consensus row names it (Singapore P3, US G3) — one exercise line on `num-times-tables` or `num-problems-data` ("share 23 among 4 — what's left over?"); (b) the mental-strategy ladder names count-on, make-ten and retrieval but not **doubles/near-doubles or compensation** — cheap additions to the within-20 exercise lines; (c) **answer-reasonableness estimation** ("will 47+25 be nearer 70 or 100?") is never asked for — one line in `num-addsub-100`, and it is the discipline the finance career's "systematic checking" claim actually rests on day-to-day.

## 2. Practice quality — up to workbook craft?

**Credit first, and it is substantial.** B18's spacing arithmetic (done once, honestly, with the Band-1 elapsed-time exception), the CR15 recognition battery restored with its provenance corrected, B15's self-checking pages, B12's flow-channel calibration, B8's grading by inference depth rather than surface size, and the B10 fading ladder are collectively *above* ordinary Singapore workbook craft — most of our commercial workbooks do not state their own retrieval arithmetic. The gap is elsewhere:

### S-4. Variation theory never landed as a design rule — IMPORTANT [CURRICULUM DOCS — framework §4]
- **Evidence.** R3 §2.5 profiles my system's "teaching by systematic **variation** (Dienes)" and the framework never carried it anywhere: "variation" has zero matches in the doctrine. What exists is adjacent but different: B7 isolates difficulties (that is *restriction*, not variation), B11's buffet gives ≥3 *parallel same-level* items (Montessori repetition — good, but unstructured), B13/GP14 vary *representation across pages* (conceptual variation's costume half — genuinely well done). What is missing is **within-set procedural variation**: the deliberate design of a practice set so that consecutive items vary one dimension systematically while the deep structure stays constant — 7+2, 7+3, 7+4 (what changed? what does it do to the answer?); 8=5+□ beside □+3=8 beside 8−5=□ as one designed contrast set, not three scattered pages. In my tradition the *item set is the lesson*; in these specs the item set's internal order is the one thing no rule governs — an author following every B-rule to the letter can still ship a page of ten unrelated correct items.
- **Fix:** one booklet rule (a natural B23): every practice set is a designed variation set — the author states what varies and what stays constant across the set, one dimension at a time; contrast sets for confusable structures are built as *adjacent items*, not just interleaved pages. Two sentences; it will quietly raise every future booklet.

### S-5. Specification asymmetry: the flagship puzzle line is specced to six levels, the numeracy booklet family to zero — IMPORTANT [CURRICULUM DOCS — deliverables ordering]
- **Evidence.** §4.4 gives the sudoku line a full applied reference (S1–S6, per-level checklists, motor ramps, provocation pages) — the document every future booklet "copies." The numeracy booklet family — carrying the strand the framework itself calls the single strongest predictor (Duncan 2007, cited in §6) and the strand S-1's word-problem ramp must live in — exists as a README bullet list ("Number bonds to 10 with pictures"). The system's own R3 §6.2 sketched the numeracy pipeline (bond diagrams at 6, bar-model pictures at 7, arrays at 8, fraction lines at 9); no document since has promoted that sketch to §4.4 grade. Meanwhile the sudoku spec runs to S5–S6 (ages 8–9+), whose booklet sessions compete for the same two weekly slots the unbuilt numeracy booklets will need. The sudoku line's *pedagogical* honesty is impeccable (soft-feed only, no gating — the anti-pattern was correctly refused); the *investment* ordering is what a Singapore department head would query.
- **Fix:** author the numeracy booklet family's applied reference (the §4.4 pattern: level table, per-level checklist, the S-1 model-method ramp, S-2's puzzler pages, S-4's variation rule exercised) as the next methodology deliverable — before extending the sudoku line past S3. One document, mostly assembled from rules that already exist.

## 3. Stretch — what does the able child get?

The system's honest answer today is: *acceleration* (mastery-not-age, run-ahead permitted, placement sweep, and since v1.6 a tier-4 decomposition trigger so a fast child does not hit sealed scrolls — that settled fix is real and I do not re-open it). Singapore's answer is different, and the difference is the finding:

### S-6. GP17 has a floor response and no ceiling response — enrichment-in-place is undesigned — IMPORTANT [CURRICULUM DOCS]
- **Evidence.** GP17/B12 rule precisely what happens *below* 70% success (different-representation corrective, one rung down — correct, Guskey-grade). Nothing rules what happens when a child cruises *above* ~95% across sets: the implicit answer is "advance," i.e. more altitude, sooner. B12's "final ~2 pages stretch" is the only depth valve, and it is two pages per month. My tradition's first response to the able child is not the next topic — it is **harder problems on the same topic**: non-routine sets, multi-step variants, prove-it-two-ways, make-the-hardest-version-you-can (the tree's own make-your-own culture is the perfect chassis and stops one rung short of this). Depth-before-acceleration also protects the system's own anti-pressure stance: a child pushed upward through tiers arrives at the R2 §9.4 fragile-motivation band with altitude instead of roots.
- **Fix:** one ruling beside GP17 (its mirror): sustained success above ~95% on a strand surfaces the *stretch variant* — S-2's non-routine set, a constrained make-your-own, a two-representations demand — before the next rung is surfaced; more volume is never the response (that half already exists in spirit in B11). Plus one sentence in §5.1's frontier-curation rule making "deep or forward?" an explicit weekly curation choice the parent owns.

### S-7. The lag lever has a ruled three-step sequence; the lead has none — NICE-TO-HAVE [CURRICULUM DOCS]
§5.4 rules exactly what happens when the child falls behind trajectory (delivery → capped dose → professional eyes). No paragraph rules the opposite case: GPC coverage a phase ahead, or the scheduled numeracy run-ahead colliding with school (a child fluent within 100 sitting through grade-1 counting lessons — the home system creates this collision by design and never addresses the school-boredom interface). One §5.4 paragraph: lead is normal (CR2 pull-not-push already says so), the response is S-6's depth-first, and the parent–teacher conversation about it is named as a thing the family does on purpose.

## 4. The bilingual strand — judged against Singapore's bilingual experience

**Credit first, because the big calls are right.** The division of labor is the correct *inversion* of Singapore's problem: our national struggle is keeping the mother tongue alive against an English-dominant environment; this family's environment supplies Estonian everywhere, so the system correctly spends its scarce home minutes on English and rides school for Estonian (CR14, the sourcing notes). The shared phonological root, the decoding-onset soft order (Estonian first, parallel-when-pulled), B21's contrast pages, B22's audio models with the §5.4 delivery ruling, and the bilingual masking watch in the atypical-development triggers — all sound, all things I have seen national systems get wrong. The receptive oral leg running hard from 5 while decoding waits is Scarborough-honest.

### S-8. The CR18 rail's English share is unruled — the §5.4 argument leans on an unpriced sub-allocation — IMPORTANT [CURRICULUM DOCS]
- **Evidence.** §5.4's dose-adequacy argument for the by-9 English endpoint rests on three legs, and leg (c) is "the oral-comprehension leg runs from 5." The delivery vehicle of that leg is the CR18 rail — ~70 min/week, priced in *parent* minutes, with the language split left as "Estonian and English alternating or stacked, family's choice." Alternating strictly means English listening gets ~35 min/week; a family drifting Estonian-heavy in a tired winter (the likeliest drift, since Estonian is the comfort language) could halve that again, and no rule or quarterly check would notice — the §5.4 trajectory monitor watches GPC coverage and decoding, not oral-leg exposure. Singapore's time-on-task lesson is blunt: the language the environment does not supply gets the protected minutes, *counted per language*, or it quietly loses them.
- **Fix:** two sentences in CR18/§5.4: an English floor on the rail (English on at least half the nights, or stacked 5+5 — the family still chooses the *pattern*), and one line in the quarterly check ("is the English rail actually running at floor?"). CR4's missed-night-is-nothing stance is untouched — this rules the design share, not a streak.

### S-9. Productive spoken English has no seed before tier 4 — IMPORTANT [CURRICULUM DOCS]
- **Evidence.** Every oral node to tier 3 is receptive by explicit design ("answers in either language"), and the settled C4 fix stages the productive rung at tier-4 decomposition — that ruling stands and I do not re-open it. But as implemented, the first time the system *invites* English out of the child's mouth (beyond mechanically reading decodables aloud) is ~age 10. Singapore's early-years experience — and the EAL literature behind our own NEL/STELLAR practice — is that low-stakes *formulaic* production belongs years earlier: songs, choral echo lines, sentence frames in games, a puppet who only speaks English for two minutes of a phonics session. Comprehension without any production runs a known risk this system is otherwise careful about: at 8–10 (exactly R2 §9.4's fragile band) speaking English for the first time becomes an identity event instead of an old habit. The respect for a silent period is right; a *standing invitation* costs nothing and is different from a demand.
- **Fix:** exercise-line-level only, no node change and no check ever: an invited-echo line on the phonics session template ("child may say it back — invited, never required"), English songs named in the rail's repertoire alongside the Estonian ones, and one B-rule sentence extending B2's register ("any English the child *volunteers* is received warmly and never corrected mid-flow"). The tier-4 rung then lands on a child who has been playing with the register for years.

## 6. The progress app — the family marks skills green/yellow/red

The documents specify a wall poster; the family additionally plans an app in which skills are marked green/yellow/red. The docs do not yet govern it, and that is itself the problem:

### A-1. The app as conceived contradicts the framework's own display law — BLOCKING [PROGRESS APP]
- **Evidence.** Three-color marking collides head-on with rulings this system fought hard for: §5.5 bans red ink and deficit display anywhere the child can see; §5.1 rules that decay *dims but never darkens* (Needs-polish keeps satisfying prerequisites; a summer holiday may dim the map, never redden it), and v1.6 *softens* dimming further for documented difficulties; CR13 rules the child sees a curated frontier of 3–5 and sealed scrolls, never the full map as a checklist wall. A naive green/yellow/red grid is precisely the artifact these rulings exist to prevent: red-as-not-yet is a red mark, a wall of grey/red future skills is the Khan free-roam demotivator CR13 names, and a three-color projection of a six-state machine (Locked → Available → Practicing → Mastered → Consolidated → Needs-polish, plus cluster partial-passes and training-ground sub-ticks) is lossy in exactly the places that carry the system's motivational design.
- **Fix (spec work, before any app ships):** a one-page display-law spec for any digital surface: (a) the state projection, stated (e.g. Consolidated = full color; Mastered/Practicing = outlined/partial like the poster's pencil ticks; Available = the surfaced-frontier subset only; Locked = not rendered; Needs-polish = a *dim* of its own color, never red, never grey-out for a child with a documented difficulty); (b) red reserved for nothing child-facing (if the parents want a triage color privately, it lives in a parent view the child never sees — §5.5's sibling-comparison ban extends to shared screens); (c) the child-facing app view obeys poster law wholesale (frontier, ceremony, capability statements), because to the child the app *is* the poster. One sentence in the framework (§5.5 or CR13) assigning poster/display rules to "any surface the child sees, paper or screen" would anchor this today.

### A-2. The app is the natural owner of §5.1's machinery — and no document assigns it; parent-marking validity must live in the data model — IMPORTANT [PROGRESS APP]
- **Evidence.** The rulings that make this system rigorous are exactly the ones a tap-to-green app deletes by default: Archetype-B instances must be one-line *described* observations (v1.6 P6 fix), keystone B-nodes need a countersign or outside-context instance, consolidation is a two-stage, dated, strip-borne mechanism with a 2-node weekly retest cap, placement sweeps mark Consolidated directly with timers started at placement date, declared pauses suspend decay, and the boss-rule/frontier checks are "tooling must scope" obligations the learning-path text explicitly hands to tooling. If the app ships as a color-picker per skill, every one of these silently degrades to a parental tap, and the P6 concern (a motivated rater) returns worse — friction removed is exactly how tallies inflate.
- **Fix:** the app spec makes the ruled mechanics *forms, not colors*: marking an Archetype-B instance requires the one-line observation; marking green requires the check-spec fields (dates, two sittings) or a placement/pause flag; the retest queue, decay timers and dim-check are computed, not hand-set. The docs' only needed change is one line in §7 of the learning path adding the app to the tooling obligations already listed.

### A-3. The app is also the cheap win the paper system is waiting for — NICE-TO-HAVE [PROGRESS APP]
Said so the spec doesn't only hear constraints: the quarterly ~20-minute dim-check, the strip-priority arithmetic (20–24 items/week, priorities 1–3), the B18 gap-rung rotation and the retest-week scheduling are clerical burdens on paper and trivial for software — an app that *generates each session's 4-item strip* from the ruled priorities would remove the single most error-prone parent duty in the system while touching nothing motivational. Build the engine before the dashboard.
