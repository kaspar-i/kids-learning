# International Review — Japanese Elementary Education Perspective

**Reviewer stance:** Japanese public-elementary tradition — lesson study (*jugyō kenkyū*), whole-child special activities (*tokkatsu*), MEXT course of study for grades 1–3. My lens: learning as membership in a community that depends on you; perseverance built by sitting *with* hard problems (*neriage* — errors surfaced and compared, not hidden); task quality over task quantity; handwriting as care (*shosha*); seasonal and nature connection as curriculum, not decoration; and character formation inseparable from academics.

**Documents read in full:** `curriculum/learning-path.md` v1.6 (including the canonical JSON), `docs/methodology/combined-framework.md` v1.6. For grounding: `docs/research/01`, `02`, `04` (targeted), `STATE.md`, the skill-tree viewer source. Read first, per instruction, so nothing settled is re-litigated: `gap-review-1-practitioner.md`, `gap-review-2-systems.md`, `gap-fix-verification.md`. **CR17 scope rulings checked before every declared gap** — each finding below either sits in territory CR17 explicitly keeps for the tree (practical life, the seasonal layer, home-shaped remainder) or concerns the system's own machinery and deliverables, which no ruling assigns elsewhere. Where a v1.6 fix already exists, I judge its *depth by Japanese standards*, not its existence.

**Classification:** every finding is tagged **CURRICULUM DOCS** or **PROGRESS-APP**, and severity-tagged blocking / important / nice-to-have. Note on the app: no app spec exists on disk — the only digital artifacts are the read-only skill-tree viewer and the booklets — so the PROGRESS-APP findings are rulings to write *now*, while they cost a paragraph instead of a rebuild.

## Overall verdict

This is the most rigorously self-audited home curriculum I have reviewed, and several of its instincts are deeply Japanese without knowing it — strategy discovery before strategy naming (S5), find-the-mistake pages as error culture, process praise scripted into footers, the refusal to pay children for learning. But seen through a Japanese elementary lens, the system educates a child who is *served by* their family rather than *serving in* it, who almost never meets a problem the machinery has not pre-calibrated them to solve, and who marks every achievement once and moves on — competence without duty, success without held struggle, completion without return. The v1.6 life stratum is real but shallow in exactly the Japanese dimension: it delivers self-management (dress yourself, tidy your things, know your address) and stops one sub-check short of contribution, at tier 0, forever. All of it is fixable inside the system's own idioms — one ladder, one weekly meeting, one page class, one ritual, one re-run rule — and none of it requires touching the graph's academic spine. The one blocking item is not in the curriculum at all: the planned family app's green/yellow/red-by-name scheme, as described, violates the framework's own motivation rulings (§5.5's red-ink and sibling-comparison bans, GP9's "not yet" framing, the softened-dimming ruling, CR13's curated frontier) and must be re-specified before a line of it is built.

**Finding counts: 1 blocking · 10 important · 3 nice-to-have.**

## 1. Responsibility and contribution — is the family the missing class community? (Q1)

Japanese children at 6 serve lunch to classmates (*kyūshoku tōban*), clean their own school daily (*o-sōji*), and hold rotating class jobs (*kakari*); by 8–9 they run class meetings and sit on school committees. The pedagogy is not the task — it is that **the group depends on you whether or not you feel like it**, on a rota, in unglamorous work, and your reliability is visible to the people you serve. Estonia's schools do not do this, and CR17 assigns civics to lasteaed — but *home contribution is exactly the territory CR17 keeps for the tree* (`sel-selfcare` is the proof), so the depth question is legitimately mine to ask.

**J-1 — IMPORTANT — CURRICULUM DOCS — The contribution strand is self-management only, and it dead-ends at tier 0.**
- What the v1.6 life stratum actually delivers: `sel-selfcare` (a) dress *yourself*, (b) tidy *your* materials, (c) one table job — the single other-facing sub-check in 108 nodes; `sel-safe-well` is self-protection; `sel-conflict-resolution` adds "takes responsibility for **own** tasks and belongings"; `ef-multistep-projects` owns "**the** backpack checklist." Every verb bends back toward the self. And the strand has no successor: after tier 0, contribution-to-others exists only as one-shot quests (restaurant night, teddy clinic) and as `sci-body-health`(d)'s care log. There is no tier-1/2/3 rung where the *scope* of the child's owned service grows — which is precisely the tokkatsu progression: table job at 6 → a standing household job at 7 → cooking one simple family element weekly at 8 → responsibility for a *person* (teaching, reading to, walking a younger child) at 9.
- The direction of service is also inverted almost everywhere: the family appears in this tree overwhelmingly as the child's **audience, customer, patient, and playtest pool** ("premiere it for the family," "a parent must solve it," "timed by a sibling," family buys at the stall, family attends the show). Restaurant night is the one true child-serves-family event — and it is a single-shot career quest, not a standing duty. In tokkatsu the arrow points the other way as a matter of weekly routine. The family *is* this system's class community; the docs have not noticed that it is one.
- **Fix (native idiom, cheap):** a `sel-contribution` continuation cluster at tier 1–2 (Archetype B, zero consumers, never gating — the exact life-stratum ruling), sub-checks shaped as a ladder: (a) holds a rotating weekly family job end-to-end (set by the family job board, not by mood); (b) prepares one simple family food element weekly by ~8; (c) does one regular service *for a person* (reads to / teaches / helps a younger child or grandparent). Extend `sel-selfcare`(b)'s tidying to shared spaces at the tier-1 rung (the child helps reset the *family's* room, not only their own materials) — the home o-sōji. One §6 SEL-row sentence naming the family as the system's community of contribution.

**J-2 — IMPORTANT — CURRICULUM DOCS — "Unprompted" is the wrong check semantics for duty.**
- Archetype B certifies dispositions via "≥3 **unprompted** instances across ≥2 weeks" (§5.2). For emotions, calming moves, conflict resolution — correct. For *jobs* it measures the wrong virtue: spontaneity. Japan builds **dependability** — the tōban child serves lunch because the rota says so, and the check of character is *reliability under the rota*, including on the days they don't feel like it. Under the current semantics, a child who does their table job every night because it is their job generates zero tally-able "unprompted instances"; a child who spontaneously helps three times and shirks the rest consolidates the node. The system's own exercise line already gestures at the right criterion ("tidies own activity materials **without prompting**", "child-owned **end to end**") — the check spec just doesn't match it.
- **Fix:** one sentence in §5.2: *for owned-job sub-checks, the tally unit is a completed rota cycle (the job done all week with ≤1 reminder), not an unprompted instance.* Applies to `sel-selfcare`(c) and everything J-1 adds. This also honestly resolves a tension the framework never names: its motivation safety consistently selects *pleasant, chosen* tasks, while duty-formation requires some required, unglamorous ones — the rota is how Japan threads that needle without rewards or nagging, and it costs GP9 nothing because nothing is paid and nothing gates.

**J-3 — IMPORTANT — CURRICULUM DOCS — No standing community reflection loop: hansei exists only as project debrief and solo review.**
- What exists: quest-manager's two-question debrief, `ef-plan-check`, `ef-goal-setting`'s "honest Friday self-review" (solo), t4's AAR habit. All either one-shot or individual. Japan's unit of reflection is the *group*: the class meeting (*gakkyūkai*) where the week is reviewed, jobs are rotated, and problems are raised by children — chaired, from surprisingly early, by children.
- **Fix:** a named **weekly family meeting** (~10 min): rotating chair — the child chairs from ~7, which is a real manager-path skill the tree currently defers to `t4-leading-teams` at 10+; each member, parents included, offers one went-well and one to-improve; next week's family jobs (J-1's rota) are assigned. Rule it the CR18 way: a named, priced, sanctioned standing item (~10 min/week, §5.6 row), exempted by name from the no-daily/no-extra-slot rules. This single mechanism gives J-1 its rota, J-6's quest debriefs somewhere to land, and the system its missing class meeting.

## 2. Productive struggle — does the machinery teach a child to sit with a hard problem? (Q2)

Credit first, honestly: S5's "named strategy taught only after months of self-found ones" is the purest Japanese sentence in these documents — a Japanese math lesson runs exactly on children's invented methods. GP17 labels struggle normal; B19's two-solutions provocation ("which do you like better?") is genuinely *neriage* in embryo; R2 itself cites productive failure. The soil is right. But the machinery, as ruled, optimizes toward smooth success:

**J-4 — IMPORTANT — CURRICULUM DOCS — The system routes around difficulty after ~2 misses; nothing anywhere asks the child to hold one hard problem across days.**
- The calibration stack: B12 ("~80% of each booklet solvable comfortably"), GP17 (below ~70% success, drop a rung with a *different* corrective, "don't repeat the page"), §5.2's check protocol ("after 2 misses, stop warmly... return ≥3 days later **with the different-representation corrective first**; no same-day retries"). Retention-optimal, motivation-safe — and it guarantees the child never has the canonical Japanese experience: **one problem, too hard for one sitting, held, returned to, and cracked** — the lesson design where a Japanese class spends 45 minutes on a single rich problem and the wrong attempts are the content. In this system, two misses end the encounter and the problem itself is swapped away. The child learns that hard problems are re-dressed until they become easy; Japan wants the child to learn that *they* persist until the problem yields. `ef-attention-persist` measures staying with a *chosen, doable* task — not the same muscle.
- **Where a Japanese educator would insert them:** (a) one **Big Problem page per booklet** from Band 2 — a puzzle *expected not to fall in one sitting* (inference one step past the band's ladder, B8's depth dial makes this easy to author), visually marked as multi-day, with a B17 footer script that forbids hints and replaces them: *"What did you try? What do we know now? Where shall we leave it for tomorrow?"* Returning to it tomorrow is the designed outcome, not a failure path. (b) The two boss milestones already carry ceremony — let each era also carry one remembered *takai mondai* the child eventually beat, attached as keystone evidence ("the problem that took me four days"). This is the single cheapest character-formation asset the system lacks.
- **One rule needs scoping, or it forbids the fix:** GP17/§5.2's "never the same page again" is correct for failed *checks* and drill pages, but as written it outlaws re-attempting the Big Problem — and the re-attempt is where the learning lives. One clause: *the corrective rule applies to checks and practice sets; a marked Big Problem page is designed for repeated return.*

**J-5 — IMPORTANT — CURRICULUM DOCS — Errors are private; the system never compares ways of thinking.**
- Control of error (GP4, B15) is Montessori's privacy of error: the page tells the child *that* it is wrong, immediately and alone. *Neriage* is the publicity of error: two solutions put side by side, the class asked *why* they disagree and *which reasoning is better*. These teach different things — self-correction vs. evaluation of reasoning — and the system has only the first. The nearest existing assets: find-the-mistake pages (spot it, name the broken rule — good, but the error is a stranger's and singular), the B19 two-solutions page (one per booklet), and `com-because-reasons`' explain-why bridge.
- **Fix:** add a **compare-two-ways page class** to B10's battery: two worked solutions to the same problem — sometimes one flawed, sometimes both valid but one more elegant — with the prompts *"Who is right? Which way do you like? Say why."* Run it as talk (the COM bridge is sitting right there), parent scripted to defend the losing method for one round so the child must argue, not just pick. Cheap, printable, and it converts the error culture from "I fix my page" to "we weigh reasoning" — which is what the lawyer, scientist and manager profiles all claim to want.

## 3. The nine one-shot quests vs. iteration culture (Q3)

**J-6 — IMPORTANT — CURRICULUM DOCS — Single-shot quests amputate the return cycle their own debriefs point at.**
- The ruling (§5.1) is self-aware: "nobody re-runs restaurant night for a checkbox" — correct, and the tree-state semantics should not change. But look at what the quest cards themselves say: quest-manager ends with *"what changes **next time**?"*; quest-entrepreneur ends with *"decide one thing **version 2 of the stall** changes"* — and by rule there is no next time and v2 never runs. The child writes hansei into a void; the debrief becomes a form. Japan's school events are the opposite design: the *same* event returns every year (*undōkai*, culture day), each cycle opened by reading the previous cycle's reflection — the event is a spiral, and children experience their own growth against a fixed format. Within-quest iteration is also uneven: `cre-invent-improve` rightly demands **two** improvement rounds, but quest-engineer and quest-youtuber run one, and quest-entrepreneur's improvement is a *decision with no consequence*.
- Pedagogical verdict on the quests as single-shot events: as *doors* they are sound — child-sized, well-gated, honestly prerequisited. As *learning cycles* they are half-built: a one-shot event with a written "next time" is a promise the system's own rules break.
- **Fix (keeps the mastery machinery untouched):** rule a **quest-revival tradition** at the poster/seasonal layer: any completed quest may be voluntarily re-run in a later season or year at bigger scale (restaurant night for grandparents; market stall at the real school fair; family court with a written rule-of-the-house that stands), the previous run's debrief sheet read aloud first. The folk-calendar layer (CR17) is the natural scheduler and gains real content by carrying it. No new node, no state change — Consolidated stays Consolidated; the tradition lives where wild nodes live. Second, align the internal loops: where a quest card writes a v2, the v2 runs (quest-entrepreneur), and week-scale quests adopt `cre-invent-improve`'s two-round floor where cheap.

## 4. Craft and care: shosha, work quality, seasonality (Q4)

**J-7 — IMPORTANT — CURRICULUM DOCS — The aesthetic bar exists for the materials and nowhere for the child's work.**
- The transcription strand is seriously built (grip → formation → automaticity, form-drawing warm-ups, B3's grid discipline) — but its telos is entirely *functional*: "automatic enough to free working memory," "speed-and-ease ticks." B6 demands the *booklets* be beautiful ("calm and beautiful, not clip-art"); no rule anywhere asks the *child* to produce one careful, beautiful thing and stand behind it. That is shosha's actual content: posture, breath, one character written with full attention, the best of three chosen *by the child* and displayed. It is also Waldorf's main-lesson book — an asset R1 documents and the framework never picked up. Quality-of-work as a value currently has zero mechanism in 108 nodes; everything is checked for correctness and fluency, nothing for care.
- **Fix (GP9-safe because the child chooses and nobody grades):** (a) a **fair-copy (seisho) ritual** at keystone evidence attachment — the work sample GP13 already requires is made as a deliberate best version, not pulled from the pile; the grandparent letter in `lit-composition` is the perfect first instance (a fair copy *because someone will receive it* — care as service, which ties to J-1). (b) One exercise line on `mot-handwriting-auto`: monthly, the child chooses "my most beautiful page" from the month's work, dates it, and it enters the portfolio — the child's own eye for quality is the thing being trained, so the child selects and adults never rank. (c) One B17 footer line on posture and unhurried strokes for form-drawing warm-ups. Three lines of text, no new node.

**J-8 — IMPORTANT — CURRICULUM DOCS — The folk-calendar layer is decorative: a scheduling claim with one consumer and no shipped vehicle.**
- CR17 names the seasonal layer "the scheduled home of cultural belonging" — but audit what actually consumes it: song repertoire joining `cre-music-play`, and prose in §3 ("an **optional light** seasonal layer"). No booklet page, no B13 Life page, no exercise anywhere is keyed to a feast or a season of print. This is the exact claim-without-delivery-vehicle pattern CR18 was written to kill (the read-aloud was "ordinary childhood" prose until a rule priced and named it) — recurring one level down, one version later. Meanwhile the tree already owns the right hooks and leaves them unwired: the weather strip runs for one week, twice, across two years (`sci-observe-senses`(c), `sci-cause-effect`) instead of returning each season; `sci-body-health`(d)'s care-for-a-living-thing log floats free of the growing season, when Japan would make it the first-grader's morning glory — planted in spring, measured all summer, seeds harvested in autumn and *given to next year's child*.
- **Fix:** (a) rule that each printed booklet carries **one B13 Life page keyed to its season of print** (vastlapäev sled-distance measuring, mardipäev mask craft, jõulud baking-by-recipe — the B13 rotation absorbs this at zero page cost); (b) make the weather strip a four-season recurrence (one week per season, same strip, compare against the last one — which is also spaced retrieval wearing seasonal clothes); (c) key the care log to spring planting where the family can. Then CR17's sentence is true.

## 5. The career-tree framing itself, and the progress app (Q5)

**The honest judgment on the framing.** Japan would never build this: careers enter Japanese schooling in middle school as *exploration of working life*, and elementary school's stated purpose is character formation inside a community — a 6-year-old's learning is never instrumentalized toward a named adult job. Having said that plainly, I must also say the documents defend the framing better than I have seen any commercial or home system do it: careers gate nothing (CR12), the child sees wonders and a curated frontier, never the DAG (CR13); the trunk union prevents the display lies; GP9/§5.5 ban every corrosive mechanic; `lit-reads-for-fun` is deliberately never rewarded; the monoculture critique is already ruled (P8's first-class tenth slot — I checked, and do not re-raise it). On paper, the wonder is lore, not teleology. The residual risk is therefore not the paper — it is **the adults' talk around the poster and the planned app's rendering of it**, and only the second has a spec-shaped hole big enough to block on.

**APP-1 — BLOCKING — PROGRESS-APP — The green/yellow/red-by-name scheme violates the framework's own motivation rulings; re-specify before build.**
- Taken as described — skills marked green/yellow/red, by name, family-visible — the app breaks at least four standing rules of the very system it would display:
  1. **Red for not-yet vs. the framework's own language.** GP9 rules failure is framed "not yet unlocked"; §5.5 bans red ink outright; CR13 renders the future as *sealed scrolls*, not deficits. A traffic light paints "not yet" as alarm. In Japanese elementary practice, unlearned material is simply *not yet taught* — no display marks a 7-year-old red for what the course of study places at 8. Locked/Available must render as unopened territory (neutral, even inviting), never as red.
  2. **By-name views vs. the sibling-comparison ban.** §5.5 bans percentile/sibling comparison; §5.6 rules one poster per child, "two posters may hang in different rooms if comparison culture threatens." A family app that lists children by name in one screen *is* the comparison dashboard the paper rules banned — rebuilt in software. Per-child views must be structurally separate; no aggregate family grid of children × skills may exist.
  3. **Three states vs. six, and the dimming ruling.** The state machine has six states, and one of them carries a hard v1.6 ruling: for a documented learning difficulty, decay renders as "neutral refresh-queued marks, **never grey-outs**" — because the dimming poster is a demoralization engine for exactly the ADHD profile. If Needs-polish maps to red or yellow, a summer holiday paints regression across the wall for the whole family; the ruling is destroyed by the palette. The app must carry the full state model with Needs-polish as a neutral maintenance mark, and the softened-dimming ruling must bind the renderer.
  4. **Child-visible scope vs. CR13.** The child's surface is 3–5 frontier nodes and one era poster; the full colored tree is an internal instrument. An app screen showing a child all 108 nodes traffic-lighted collapses CR13's central design decision in one glance.
- **Fix:** before any build, write the app's charter as rulings (one page): states derive **only** from check events in the evidence log (never from adult impression — see APP-2); palette = achieved / in-progress / *unopened* with no alarm color for not-yet and a neutral refresh mark for decay; per-child spaces with no cross-child grid; child mode = the poster metaphor (their era, their frontier, their coloring), parent mode = the full tree, alone. The paper system spent five loops earning these protections; the app must inherit them, not reset them.

**APP-2 — IMPORTANT — PROGRESS-APP — "Marked by name" must not mean "marked by hand": adult-set colors would bypass the check machinery and invert ownership.**
- If parents set green/yellow/red by judgment, the app silently replaces §5.2's entire check apparatus (archetypes, two-stage consolidation, the v1.6 tally-validity guard-rails against exactly this motivated rater) with parental impression — reopening P6 one abstraction layer up. And GP13 is explicit that the child owns the record: the *child* colors the poster on evidence; assessment done *to* the child in an adult-held dashboard is a surveillance artifact, not documentation. Japan's version of this value: the record of learning lives in the child's own carefully kept notebook, and the public wall shows the *class's* shared work, not ranked individuals.
- **Fix:** the app writes state only from logged check events (§5.3's evidence log is already the right backbone); any manual override requires a logged evidence line; the child-facing act of marking (coloring the node) stays the child's, app or paper.

**APP-3 — IMPORTANT — PROGRESS-APP — The app must carry the ruled ceremonies and protections, or it will out-compete the poster and strip them.**
- A glanceable app will inevitably become the family's primary surface; if it ships without the poster's ruled texture — unlock ceremonies (CR4's signing), keystone evidence attachment, the era ceremony, declared-pause timer suspension (§5.6: the poster *never dims during a pause*), placement-sweep batch entry — then the most-used artifact will be the one with the fewest protections. Either the app implements the ceremony-and-pause layer or it is explicitly subordinate (a parent logbook, with the wall poster ruled primary and said so in the app itself).

**J-10 — NICE-TO-HAVE — CURRICULUM DOCS — Career-identity talk is unscripted, and wonder abandonment has no display ruling.**
- B17 scripts hint language and praise language with citations — but nothing scripts the sentence every parent will face: *"I'm going to be a doctor"* / *"I'm bad at the engineer path."* The praise literature the framework cites (Mueller & Dweck) has a direct analogue for identity labels: praise the pull, never affirm the label ("you love finding out how bodies work" — not "our little doctor"). And CR12's own premise is that interests churn — yet no ruling says what an *abandoned* wonder looks like on the poster or in the app (a fold-away map corner, never a failure mark). One B17-class parent card plus one poster-spec sentence. Cheap insurance on the framing's one real conversational risk.

## 6. Remaining nice-to-haves

**J-9 — NICE-TO-HAVE — CURRICULUM DOCS — The parent never models being wrong.** The system scripts parents as hint-givers and process-praisers; Japanese lesson-study culture rests on adults publicly studying their *own* practice. Home version, one B17 line + one J-3 agenda item: the parent narrates one own mistake and fix aloud weekly ("I planned the shop trip badly — next time I'll write the list first"), and takes their own to-improve turn at the family meeting. Children believe hansei when they see adults do it.

**J-11 — NICE-TO-HAVE — CURRICULUM DOCS — The child never gives back on the rail, and rarely faces a non-household audience.** CR18's read-aloud rail is parent→child throughout; from ~8 (`lit-reading-fluency`), one rail night a week can be child-reads-to-younger-sibling-or-grandparent (*yomikikase* by children) — fluency practice, service (J-1), and appetite (I-6's territory) in one unpriced move. Similarly `com-present`/quest premieres could name one non-household audience instance (grandparents, huviring) before tier-4's real audiences — Japan's *happyōkai* instinct: performing for people who did not watch you rehearse.

## 7. Checked and NOT gaps (credit, and rulings honored)

- **Strategy discovery before naming** (S5, months of self-found strategies first) — this *is* Japanese structured problem-solving; protect it fiercely at decomposition time.
- **Find-the-mistake as debugging culture** (B16), **two-solutions provocation** (B19), **explain-why-the-cell** (COM bridge) — real neriage seeds; J-5 grows them rather than inventing anything.
- **No streaks, no comparison, no red ink, informational rewards only** (GP9, CR4, §5.5) — stronger anti-corruption machinery than most Japanese commercial juku products; the app findings exist precisely because this paper standard deserves inheriting.
- **Form drawing opening every booklet** — shūji's cousin; with J-7's care bar it becomes the real thing.
- **Music depth** — `cre-music-play` is small, but CR17 rules muusika proper to lasteaed; by the division of labor as ruled, the depth is adequate. No finding.
- **Wonder-set monoculture, autonomy gradient, rater validity, tier-4 vacuum** — all settled in the gap reviews and verified fixed or ruled; not re-raised (APP-2 touches P6 only in the new app context).
- **Restaurant night, table job, care-for-a-living-thing** — genuine service seeds, honestly credited; J-1's complaint is that they are one-shot or tier-0-only, never a ladder.
- **The koolivalmidus and CR17 honesty rewrites** — the corrected coverage claims are exemplary; J-8 asks only that the seasonal half of CR17 ship a vehicle.
