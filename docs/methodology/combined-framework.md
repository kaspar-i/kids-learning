## 1. Guiding principles (merged, deduplicated)

The four reports converge on a small set of load-bearing principles. Each appears in at least two reports; none is contradicted by any (contradictions live in Section 2).

**GP1 — Mastery gates progression; age never does.** Advancement happens when a prerequisite is demonstrably mastered, not when a birthday or calendar page turns. Age bands are difficulty *calibration*, not gates. [R1 TR1; R2 r1 (Bloom, Duncan 2007, Piaget-revised domain-specificity); R3 §0.1 ("build the order from the consensus, treat age as a tunable band"); R4 A1, C3]

**GP2 — Skill type determines method.** Three registers, applied by what is being learned, not by ideology:
- *Biologically secondary skills* (English decoding, math facts, letter formation): short, explicit, direct instruction with spaced retrieval — dressed in play but structurally drill. [R1 §7.2, §7.4; R2 §10, r20–21; R3 §5 phonics row]
- *Conceptual understanding* (place value, patterns, logic, science ideas): guided play — adult sets the goal and environment, child directs within it. [R1 §7.1, §7.4 (Skene 2022, Alfieri 2011); R3 §5 start-age row]
- *Dispositions* (curiosity, persistence, self-checking): free choice, control of error, autonomy. [R1 §7.4; R2 §9 (SDT)]

**GP3 — Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract (CPA), per skill, always.** Objects to place → pictures to draw/circle → symbols to write. This is simultaneously Montessori's materialized abstractions, Froebel's solid→surface→line→point dial, Bruner's enactive–iconic–symbolic, and Singapore's CPA spine. Symbolic versions come *after* the concrete version is easy, never instead of it. [R1 BR3, §2.1, §5.4; R2 r13, §1.2; R3 §2.5, §6.2; R4 A1]

**GP4 — Control of error: every exercise self-checks.** The child, not the adult, discovers and fixes errors — via unique-solution puzzles, fold-over answers, checksum pictures. The adult is never "grader." Feedback must be immediate, which children specifically need for retrieval to work. [R1 BR1 (Montessori); R2 r8 (Fazio & Marsh); R4 A1 (deliberate practice feedback loops)]

**GP5 — Respect the working-memory budget.** ≤3 things to hold in mind at 5–6, ≤4 at 8–9; one instruction line; one new element per exercise; externalize state onto the page (symbol banks, scratch boxes, tick marks). Difficulty is graded by inference depth / interacting elements, not by surface size. [R2 §3, r2, r6, r12 (Cowan, Gathercole, Sweller); R1 §1 baseline table; R4 A1]

**GP6 — Isolate one difficulty at a time; automate the bottom of the tree.** Never a new format and a new content skill together. Fluency at the base (counting, letter–sound, number bonds) is pursued precisely because automaticity frees WM for everything above. [R1 BR2 (isolation of difficulty); R2 §3.2, r6; R4 A1]

**GP7 — The canonical learning loop: explore → worked example → faded practice → create.** Free-ish exploration first (teaching too early narrows discovery — Bonawitz), then one worked example showing *rules not strategies*, then completion problems with fading scaffolds, then recognition before production (~3:1), then "make your own." [R1 BR4, §7.3, §7.4 ("play → instruct → play"); R2 r3–r4 (worked-example, completion, expertise-reversal); R4 A1 (scaffolding)]

**GP8 — Space it, retrieve it, interleave it.** Sessions short and frequent (10–20 min/day beats one weekly block); every session opens with ~2 minutes of retrieval of older material with immediate self-check; new skills get one blocked page then mix with confusable older skills forever; mastered content revisits at expanding gaps (~1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 1 quarter) and decays visibly if unmaintained. [R2 §5–7, r7–r10; R1 BR9 (Waldorf block-and-revisit aligns with spacing); R4 A1, C4 (Khan mastery challenges)]

**GP9 — Protect intrinsic motivation structurally.** Autonomy (child picks among the 3–5 surfaced frontier options, §5.1), competence (progress shown as capability: "you can now solve 6×6"), relatedness (parent roles, signing ceremonies). Never pay for activity: no per-page stickers/points, no streaks, no percentile or sibling comparison, no red ink. Rewards are informational (mark new competence), never controlling. Failure is "not yet unlocked." [R1 BR12, TR10, §7.3 (overjustification); R2 §9, r15–r18 (SDT, Deci meta-1999, Stipek optimism-protection); R4 A5 (Hamari; anti-patterns list)]

**GP10 — Two literacy tracks, one phonological root.** Estonian decoding is near-free (transparent orthography, school delivers it at 7); English decoding needs a 2–3-year systematic synthetic phonics program the school will not supply. Shared root: oral phonemic awareness. English *language comprehension* (vocabulary, read-alouds, listening) starts hard at 5; English *decoding* instruction from ~6–7. Materials for ages 5–7 must never require English decoding. **Decoding-onset order (the one uniquely bilingual sequencing question, ruled):** frontier curation surfaces `lit-ee-decoding` before `lit-en-phonics-1` — R2 §10's "explicit English reading is sensible after Estonian decoding is underway" — as a *soft curation order only, never a prerequisite edge*; parallel onset is acceptable when the child pulls, because the early GPC sets barely overlap and every clashing letter value gets a B21 contrast page. **The rejected lighter path, named (v1.7, FIN-15):** a Nordic exposure route to English exists — oral-first school English from ~7–9, subtitled media, no early phonics — costing ~zero desk minutes and reaching a comparable endpoint ~3–4 years later; this family chose the by-9 endpoint, and that *choice*, not necessity alone, is what prices English's two weekly sessions — written down so a future tired-winter "do we drop phonics?" decision meets the honest trade-off instead of a silence. [R2 §10, r19–r20 (Seymour 2003, Castles/Rastle/Nation); R3 §0.3, §5 phonics row, §6.1; B21]

**GP11 — Play register at 5–6, formal register earned at 7+.** The late-starting systems (Estonia, Finland, Singapore) outperform early-starting ones; early academic pressure is negative-EV (Marcon, Durkin, Suggate). Ages 5–6 look like structured play; school register begins at 7 — matching the Estonian school start exactly. Same underlying skill graph, two "skins." [R1 §1, §7.3, TR4; R2 §9.4; R3 §0.2, §5 start-age row]

**GP12 — One common trunk; careers are horizon lore.** ~80% of the age 5–9 foundations for all 9 target careers are shared (literacy, numeracy, EF, attention, oral communication, cooperation, persistence — R4 B2 mapped the original eight; the ninth, the v1.5 content-creator career, draws on the same shared trunk plus the COM/CRE/DIG chains already in the map). Careers differentiate through upper-tier weighting and themed quests, never through early forks — career capstones are visible-but-greyed "wonders" from day 1, unlockable from ~tier 3 (age 8+). The trunk is **named** (the learning-path's `common_trunk` node list) and unioned into every career's highlighted path: the ~80% sharing is a scheduling-and-display guarantee — every child walks the whole trunk regardless of which wonder pulls them — never an emergent property of prerequisite edges (career closures alone are career-specific by design). [R4 B2, C1, C3.7; R1 TR7; R3 §6.1]

**GP13 — Learning made visible; the child owns the record.** The skill tree is a child-facing wall poster the child colors/stamps, with evidence attached (work sample, dictated sentence per keystone node); booklets carry documentation surfaces (date, self-portrait, "my thinking" margin) and end with child-made artifacts. Assessment and motivation are the same object. **Keystone nodes are defined and tagged** (so the poster and booklet pipelines can implement evidence attachment): a keystone is (a) a boss milestone, (b) a career capstone quest node, or (c) an era-capping chain-end of a branch's tier-3 line (a branch with multiple legs caps each leg — literacy's Estonian comprehension, Estonian composition and English reading; numeracy's problems-and-data and fractions legs) — tagged `keystone: true` in the learning-path graph data, and **the tag, not a recomputation, is normative** (micro-nodes — `dig-typing`, `num-chance`, `dig-media-sense` — dispositional observation nodes — `lit-reads-for-fun` and the life-stratum clusters, where attaching ceremony or evidence would violate GP9's ban on paying for a disposition — and mid-chain nodes are deliberately excluded even where a mechanical chain-end rule would catch them). Evidence attaches at keystones by default and nowhere else by default; a family may always attach more. [R1 TR5, BR10, §3.4 (Waldorf child-made book), §4.4–4.5 (Reggio documentation); R4 A5 (Open Badges evidence log, signing ceremony)]

**GP14 — Same deep skill in many costumes.** Transfer is not automatic (horizontal décalage); every reasoning skill appears in ≥2–3 surface forms (sudoku rule ≈ seating-chart puzzle ≈ color-grid puzzle), and a concept is not "done" until met in ≥2 representations. [R2 r24; R1 §4.4 (Reggio hundred languages), BR5 (Froebel Life/Beauty/Knowledge)]

**GP15 — Paper is the correct medium for 5–7, not a compromise.** Handwriting activates reading circuitry (James & Engelhardt); fine motor at school entry predicts later math/reading (Grissmer). Pencil-on-paper mechanics (tracing → copying → free writing), grid work, and form-drawing warm-ups are first-class strands. Screens enter via the digital branch on its own schedule. [R3 §3.5, §6.2; R1 BR8, §5.4 (Froebel grid work), §3.4 (form drawing)]

**GP16 — EF and SEL are trunk, not decoration.** Self-regulation at 5–6 predicts academics better than IQ (Blair & Razza, Diamond, Duncan, Durlak +11 percentile points, Moffitt/Dunedin). EF/SEL get real nodes with real checks. **How "trunk" is operationalized — through session design and observation, not prerequisite fan-out:** every session is a games-with-rules workout (sudoku included; §6's own EF row: "trained as by-product of engaging, gradually harder rule-games — never drilled directly"), plan-do-check surfaces ride every booklet, and the EF chain sits in the named `common_trunk` on every career's highlighted path. The graph deliberately gives EF nodes only a few hard out-edges (project-shaped consumers such as iteration and team roles): with fan-in capped at 2, wiring EF as a prerequisite of everything is arithmetically impossible — and undesirable, since it would lock the whole map behind dispositional tallies. EF is trained through games-with-rules, never through abstract "brain training." [R2 §3, r23; R3 §3.4, §6.1; R4 B1, B2, C1]

**GP17 — Target ~70–90% success; struggle is normal and labeled.** The flow channel for children sits around 80% success; below ~70%, drop a ladder rung with a *different* corrective, don't repeat the page. Mixed/harder pages carry a note to the parent that slower-and-harder is the point (desirable difficulties). **The ceiling response (v1.7, GP17's mirror — the floor had a ruled response and the ceiling had none):** sustained success above ~95% on a strand surfaces the **stretch variant** before the next rung — a non-routine puzzler set (B24), a constrained make-your-own, a prove-it-two-ways or two-representations demand — and **more volume is never the response**. Depth precedes acceleration for the able child: the next rung is surfaced when depth is exhausted, not when speed is displayed, and §5.1's frontier curation owns the weekly "deep or forward?" choice. **Scope of the drop-a-rung corrective (v1.7):** it applies to failed *checks* and *practice sets*; a marked multi-day Big Problem page (B24) is *designed* for repeated return and is exempt — re-attempting it is the intended outcome, never a failure path. [R2 r11, r18; R4 A1 (Csikszentmihalyi), A1 (Guskey: correctives differ from original instruction); SGP S-6; JPN J-4]

**GP18 — Depth over range in early number.** Prioritize subitising, part-whole/number bonds, cardinality and comparison to 10 over big-number rote counting (the UK 2021 revision, Singapore practice, and Estonian modesty all point the same way). [R3 §5 counting row, §2.3; R2 §11]

## 2. Conflicts between methodologies — and the ruling on each

The reports surface genuine contradictions. Each gets an explicit ruling (CR#) so downstream work never re-litigates them.

### CR1 — Montessori/discovery vs. direct instruction (Kirschner–Sweller–Clark)
**Conflict.** R1 §2 (Montessori: child-directed choice, auto-education) vs. R1 §7.2 (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark: minimal guidance fails novices; Project Follow Through: Direct Instruction won on skills *and* self-esteem).
**Ruling.** Method follows skill type (GP2). Explicit instruction is mandatory for English phonics, math-fact fluency, and letter formation — delivered in short doses inside a playful frame. Guided play is the default for concepts. Free choice governs *which* unlocked activity, never *whether* the explicit strands exist. The skill tree flags the two explicit strands visually ("training grounds" vs. "exploration territories") so parents run them differently. [R1 §7.4, TR9; R2 r20–21]

### CR2 — Waldorf's delayed academics (age 7) vs. Montessori's early academics (reading and 4-digit math at 5)
**Conflict.** R1 §3 (Waldorf: no academics before ~7) vs. R1 §2.2 (Montessori: movable-alphabet writing and golden-bead arithmetic at 5–6 are normal); England demands decoding at 5 while Estonia/Finland wait until 7 and win anyway (R3 §1, §5).
**Ruling.** Follow the *pull, don't push* compromise: academic content is **available** early Montessori-style (through materials, tiles, games — CPA stage "concrete") but **demanded** never; the formal register waits for 7 (GP11). One hard exception: English phonics starts ~6–7 by the clock, because English's opaque orthography needs the 2.5-year runway and no school will provide it (GP10) — a **UK-style SSP sequence begun at 6–7 by our own ruling** (UK Reception children start statutory SSP at 4–5 and decode CVC words by 5, so ours is a deliberately delayed start reaching the same endpoint by ~9, not "the UK clock"). CR14 names this and the numeracy consensus clock as the two *scheduled* run-aheads of the Estonian calendar and distinguishes both from mastery-driven drift, which is permitted but never scheduled. [R1 §1, §7.4; R3 §0.3, §5, §6.1; R2 §10.2]

### CR3 — Play vs. structured practice
**Conflict.** R1 §7.3 (Bonawitz: demonstration suppresses exploration; Marcon/Durkin: early formality backfires) vs. R1 §7.2 and R2 §5–7 (retrieval, spacing, interleaving *require* worksheet-like structured practice).
**Ruling.** Sequence them: **play → instruct → play** is the arc of every booklet and every node (GP7). Exploration precedes the worked example (protecting discovery); structured practice follows it (securing retention); creative/open use closes it (securing ownership). Guided play is the *default register*; structured practice is a *scheduled dose inside the session*, never the whole diet — up to ~10 min of the 10–15-min session at 5–6 (training-ground days only), rising to ~15 min of the 20-min session by 8–9. CR16 rules the session totals; the §3 weekly template fixes which sessions carry which dose. [R1 §7.4, BR4; R2 r3, r7; CR16]

### CR4 — Intrinsic motivation (overjustification) vs. gamification (badges, XP, skill tree)
**Conflict.** R1 §7.3 and R2 §9 (Lepper 1973; Deci meta-analysis: expected tangible task-contingent rewards corrode intrinsic motivation) vs. the entire premise of R4 (a Civ-style tree with unlocks, badges, eurekas).
**Ruling.** Gamify **information, not payment** (R4 A5, Hamari): the tree marks competence growth (levels, unlocked abilities, visible map filling in), which SDT *supports*, and bans the controlling variants — no points-for-time-on-task, no per-page tokens, no streaks (loss-aversion engines are inappropriate at 5–9; a family trip must never feel like losing), no badges for attendance, no leaderboards. Rewards that survive: node unlock ceremonies (parent signs the poster), saga/era completions, new puzzle *families* unlocking (Froebel-style numbered Gifts), Eureka discounts for real-world quests. [R1 TR10, BR12; R2 r15; R4 A5, C4 anti-patterns]

### CR5 — Waldorf's anti-technology stance vs. the digital branch and software-engineer career
**Conflict.** R1 §3.3 (Waldorf delays screens/media; "anti-technology stance conflicts with a software-engineer career branch if taken literally") vs. R3 §3.6 (Estonia's ProgeTiiger puts Bee-Bots in kindergarten; England makes computing statutory at Y1) and R4 B1 (Bers: ages 4–7 do real sequencing/debugging).
**Ruling.** Take Waldorf's *screen* caution, not its *computation* caution. The digital chain is unplugged-first: sequencing picture cards and "robot game" commands (5) → arrow-programs/Bee-Bot (5–6) → introductory block coding (6–7; canonically ScratchJr, but any equivalent block environment satisfies the node — R4 C3.4 activity-agnosticism) → Scratch (8–9). Computational thinking at 5–6 happens on paper and floors, no screen required — which also keeps GP15 intact. Screens enter deliberately at the block-coding node, framed and co-used. [R3 §3.6, §4 digital chain; R4 B1, B3; R1 §3.3]

### CR6 — Reggio's emergent curriculum vs. a fixed prerequisite DAG
**Conflict.** R1 §4 (Reggio: curriculum emerges from the child's questions; deliberately no fixed sequence, no assessment instruments) vs. R2 r1/R4 A6 (explicit prerequisite graph with unlock criteria).
**Ruling.** The graph stays rigorous *internally*; Reggio governs the *surface and the margins*: (a) **wild nodes** — pre-printed blank nodes the child claims for self-chosen skills, connected with soft edges, legitimizing interests we didn't predict; (b) one **provocation page** per booklet (open-ended prompt with a strong constraint, never a blank page); (c) child voice on every completed keystone node ("what I can do now / what surprised me"); (d) the tree itself is documentation, family-facing and child-colored. Emergence gets ~10–15% of the territory; the trunk stays engineered. [R1 §4.4–4.5, TR5, TR8; R4 A2 ("the prerequisite graph and the map you show are two different products")]

### CR7 — Narrative richness (Waldorf) vs. clean working areas (cognitive load theory) — and Montessori's anti-fantasy stance
**Conflict.** R1 §3 (Waldorf: every concept enters through story and image) vs. R2 §4 (seductive details and decorative clutter measurably harm learning) vs. R1 §2.3 (classic Montessori discourages pretend/fantasy, which modern science values).
**Ruling.** **Story between exercises, never inside the grid.** Each booklet gets a thin narrative wrapper (recurring cast, light arc) living on covers, transition pages and reward pages; the working area stays Montessori-spare — no cartoon noise near the puzzle, labels integrated into the graphic, decoration quarantined. We reject Montessori's fantasy ban (modern evidence favors pretend play) but adopt its visual asceticism where cognition happens. [R1 BR6, §2.4, §3.4; R2 r5]

### CR8 — Blocked practice (Waldorf blocks, Montessori repetition) vs. interleaving evidence
**Conflict.** R1 §3.1 (Waldorf main-lesson blocks: one subject for weeks) and R1 §2.1 (Montessori voluntary repetition to internal "doneness") vs. R2 §7 (Rohrer: interleaving beats blocking at delay, d ≈ 1.0).
**Ruling.** The literature itself contains the truce: **block briefly when a skill is brand new** (novices need it — CLT), **then interleave it with confusable older skills forever after**. At series scale, adopt Waldorf's block-and-revisit (go deep, rest it, return one level harder a month later) — which is spacing wearing Waldorf clothes. Honor Montessori repetition as the parallel-difficulty buffet: ≥3 same-level puzzles before any step up, child chooses order. At 5–6, blocking dominates; interleaving ramps at 7+. [R2 r10, §7 caveats; R1 BR9, BR11, §3.4]

### CR9 — Sensitive periods (Montessori) vs. modern skepticism
**Conflict.** R1 §2.1 (sensitive periods as windows of near-effortless learning) vs. R1 §2.3 ("directionally supported but the precise windows are clinical observation, not modern neuroscience").
**Ruling.** Render sensitive periods as **glow windows, never locks**: nodes advertise "cheap XP" during their window (sound–symbol mapping 4.5–6, grip/motor 4–6, times-table automatization 7–9, second-language ease all of 5–9) but remain completable any time. Held loosely, used for scheduling nudges only. [R1 TR3, §2.5]

### CR10 — Whole-to-parts (Waldorf) vs. incremental parts-to-whole
**Conflict.** R1 §3.2 (Waldorf: 12 = □ + □ before 3 + 4 = ?; all four operations introduced together) vs. the incremental staircases of every national curriculum (R3 §4).
**Ruling.** The staircase orders the *tree*; whole-to-parts flavors the *pages*. Keep the consensus numeracy chain as dependency structure, but systematically include inverted formats: missing-addend problems (8 = 5 + □) from the start, "find the wrong cell in this finished grid," equals-sign-as-balance throughout — which R2 independently mandates (McNeil: huge later-algebra dividend). [R1 §3.4; R2 r22; R3 §4 numeracy chain]

### CR11 — Assessment cultures: test-free (Finland/Estonia/Reggio) vs. mastery checks (Bloom/Khan/ALEKS)

### CR14 — Estonian grade anchoring vs. the home head start
**Conflict.** Estonian children enter grade 1 in the autumn of the year they turn 7 (R3 §1), so grade 1 spans ~7–8, grade 2 ~8–9 and grade 3 ~9–10 — yet v1.0 labeled bands 2–4 (6–7, 7–8, 8–9) as grades 1–3, silently running the whole system a year ahead of the school it claimed to anchor to, contradicting CR2's "phonics is the single deliberate run-ahead" and straining GP11/GP18's anti-acceleration stance. The first boss gate (fluent +/− within 20 at ~7) also landed before school entry, where *koolivalmidus* expects only +/− within 5.
**Ruling.** Grade labels shift one year later, everywhere: Band 2 (6–7) = **koolieelik** (final lasteaed year); Band 3 (7–8) = **grade 1**; Band 4 (8–9) = **grade 2**; the Estonian grade-3 outcome list is the external checkpoint for **~9–10** (early Voyager). The *koolivalmidus* profile remains the age-6/7 anchor. Where the mastery ladder outruns school anyway — e.g. a child consolidating +/− within 20 during the koolieelik year — that is *pull, not push* (CR2, GP1) operating as designed: content is available early, demanded never, and boss gates carry **no deadline** (completable during grade 1 is normal and fine). **Two strands are *scheduled* ahead of the Estonian curriculum, both by explicit ruling — and their warrants are different in kind, stated honestly since v1.7 (FIN-9):** (1) **English literacy**, *evidence-mandated*: the opaque orthography needs the 2–3-year runway, school will not supply it, and the by-9 endpoint is unreachable without a program (CR2, GP10 — itself a deliberately delayed start relative to UK Reception's statutory 4–5); (2) **numeracy**, ***evidence-permitted and family-chosen*** — the earlier "evidence-endorsed" overstated it: R3 §6's Implications do recommend the UK/Singapore number clock, but that recommendation sits beside R3's own headline finding that the late-starting systems — Estonia included, Europe's top PISA performer — outperform the early starters *on the slower clock*, so the run-ahead is a preference the evidence permits, not one it mandates. The scheduled clock places the Estonian grade-3 outcome list inside tier 3 (8–9) as *available target content* while the list remains the **~9–10 legal checkpoint** — mastery-gating is the pressure valve, so completing that content across the tier-3/4 boundary is normal and no deadline exists (the same stance the boss gates carry). **And the Band-2 number dose is pull-conditional (v1.7):** the §3 template's two number-led koolieelik sessions run only while the child is pulling number work; absent pull they revert to booklet/free-choice sessions, with the frontier still surfacing number roots — CR2's "available early, demanded never" extended from content to *time*, so the compromise is honored for both. Every other strand follows the Estonian calendar. [R3 §1, §2.1, §6, §7; GP1, GP11, GP18; CR2; FIN-9]

### CR18 — The daily read-aloud: simultaneously mandatory and forbidden
**Conflict.** §3 (binding) ruled that daily prescriptions do not exist outside the ~2-minute strips and filed read-alouds under "ordinary childhood" — while three trunk nodes (`lit-en-oral-vocab`, `lit-en-oral-2`, `lit-en-oral-3`) prescribe *daily* English read-alouds with mastery checks, and §5.4's dose-adequacy argument for the by-9 endpoint leans explicitly on the oral leg running from 5. Either the read-aloud was system work (violating §3 and unpriced in §5.6) or ordinary childhood (leaving the trunk's oral leg with no scheduled delivery vehicle at all). The likeliest real-world outcome was the worst one: it silently stops in busy months and nobody notices until a check fails (gap review 1, blocking).
**Ruling.** The **family read-aloud rail**: ~10 minutes daily, Estonian and English alternating or stacked, family's choice — the **single sanctioned daily item outside the session system, exempted by name** from §3's no-daily-prescriptions rule. It is priced honestly in §5.6 (~70 min/week of parent time, on top of the session rows), named in the §3 template, and is the delivery vehicle the `lit-en-oral-*` nodes ride — their cards say so. The rail carries only reading and talk; mastery checks on the oral nodes run inside sessions, never on the rail. The Estonian bedtime read-aloud is the same protected ritual and the soil of `lit-reads-for-fun`. On the rail, "daily" means the family's normal: a missed night is nothing — no streaks, no make-ups (CR4) — and the rail predates and outlives the system. **The English floor (v1.7, SGP S-8):** the language split is the family's choice in *pattern*, ruled in *share* — English carries **at least half the nights, or a stacked ~5+5 every night** — because the oral-comprehension leg is leg (c) of §5.4's dose-adequacy argument and English is the one language the environment does not supply, so its minutes are counted, not hoped for (the likeliest drift is Estonian-heavy tired winters, and no rule would have noticed); the quarterly check (§5.4) asks whether the floor is actually running. A missed night remains nothing — this rules the design share, never a streak. **The repertoire invites production (v1.7, SGP S-9):** English songs sit in the rail repertoire beside the Estonian ones, and any English the child *volunteers* is received warmly and never corrected mid-flow (B2) — a standing invitation, never a demand, so the tier-4 productive rung lands on an old habit instead of an identity event. **The return leg (v1.7, JPN J-11):** from ~8 (`lit-reading-fluency`), one rail night a week may flip direction — the child reads to a younger sibling or a grandparent — fluency practice, service (`sel-contribution`(d)) and appetite in one unpriced move; the parent's read-aloud nights continue. [GP8; GP10; CR4; §5.4; §5.6; gap review 1; SGP S-8/S-9; JPN J-11]

### CR19 — The family progress app vs. the display law (the app charter)
**Conflict.** The family's planned progress app marked skills green / yellow / **red** by name. All three international reviews blocked on the same point (FIN-1–5, SGP A-1–3, JPN APP-1–3): a child-visible "not learned" red state converts the wonder-map into a deficit report — a 6-year-old before a 109-node tree would face a self-portrait that is mostly red *for years, by design* — and violates the framework's own standing law four ways (GP9's "not yet", §5.5's red-ink ban, §5.1's decay-dims-never-darkens, CR13's curated frontier), while no ruling bound the paper display law to screens.
**Ruling — the app charter, binding for any digital family surface, current or future.**
1. **Additive palette only; the red/"not learned" state is removed entirely** (decided by the family and already implemented in the app): the states are *unmarked* ("not started yet" — neutral, sealed-scroll territory, never a deficit), *learning*, and *mastered* — **for parents and children alike**. No negative state exists anywhere child-visible, and **red is reserved for nothing child-facing**; if a triage color is ever wanted it may mark parent-only *system obligations* (an overdue retest queue, a decomposition trigger firing), never a child's skill — and a family app is assumed child-visible regardless of view flags: phones are shared, shoulders are looked over.
2. **The paper poster remains primary; the app mirrors it, never replaces it.** Mastery is celebrated at the wall — the child colors, the parent signs (GP13's ceremony, CR4's unlock rituals) — and the app records what the wall already shows; child-facing celebration stays physical, and declared-pause protection (§5.6) binds the app exactly as it binds the poster. **Marking is done with or by the child wherever feasible** (GP13: the child owns the record) — an app entry is a transcription of the wall, not an adult's verdict delivered over the child's head.
3. **CR13's curated-frontier law extends to screens by name:** era-scoped default view (~one era), frontier-first navigation, tiers beyond current+1 rendered as sealed scrolls, careers rendered as wonders — **never as progress bars** — and the internal DAG never shown to the child (the learning path's "the child never sees this file" applies to screens too). An app's natural affordances — scroll everything, expand everything, update instantly — are the Khan free-roam anti-pattern CR13 exists to prevent, with better production values.
4. **No aggregate metrics, no attention mechanics:** no "X of N mastered," no per-era completion percent, and **"% toward a career" is never shown to a child** (the instrumental framing CR12 exists to prevent — a fraction toward Doctor turns a wonder into a grind); no push notifications or reminders (a reminder is a streak engine in disguise — CR4 banned the mechanism, not the pixel; the parent's quarterly dim-check is the only sanctioned cadence); no visible session timers (CR16's dose arithmetic belongs to the parent); one profile per child and **no cross-child grid or shared comparative screen** (§5.6's multi-child poster ruling extended to information architecture).
5. **No live decay display:** Needs-polish rendering appears only when the parent runs the quarterly dim-check sweep — a button, not a daemon — declared pauses suspend it (§5.6 re-entry protocol), and §5.4's softened-dim rendering for documented difficulties is the **default code path**, not an option.
6. **Source-of-truth honesty (an accepted v1 simplification, stated rather than papered over):** the current app's states are **hand-set by the family, not derived from logged check events.** That is ruled acceptable *for v1 because the app is a mirror* (clause 2): the §5.2 checks and the §5.3 evidence log remain the actual source of truth for every state, and a hand-set mark is a transcription of what the wall and the log already establish — never itself an assessment act. Any future version that computes states must derive them from the evidence log (a mastered mark requires the check-spec fields — dates, two sittings — or a placement/pause flag; an Archetype-B instance requires its one-line described observation), so §5.2's tally-validity guard-rails apply one layer up rather than silently dissolving into a color-picker.
7. **Build the engine before the dashboard:** the app's unambiguous value-add is the parent-side ops the documents already assign to tooling — retest queues, dim-check sweeps, placement sweeps, generating each session's 4-item strip from the §5.1 priorities — the half with no developmental risk.
8. **Stated v1 deviation — the parent-operated full view.** The shipped v1 app renders the whole tree (Overview) and whole career paths on demand: a deliberate, owner-decided deviation from clause 3's era-scoping, ruled acceptable because the v1 app is a **parent-operated planning surface** — the screen equivalent of this file and the learning path, which CR13 already assigns to parent territory — used *with* the child in the clause-2 marking ritual, not handed to the child for free-roam. The career summary shows only additive gathered-counts (clause 4 honored: no ratios, percentages, or bars). Any future child-driven mode (the child navigating alone) must implement clause 3's era-scoped, sealed-scroll rendering before it ships; this clause never licenses that.
[FIN-1–5; SGP A-1–3; JPN APP-1–3; GP9; GP13; CR4; CR11; CR12; CR13; CR16; §5.1; §5.4; §5.5; §5.6]

### CR20 — Individual mastery machinery vs. the missing community loop (the weekly family meeting)
**Conflict.** Every reflection instrument in the system is individual or one-shot — plan-do-check, the solo Friday goal review, quest debriefs written into a void — and §3/§5.6 rightly ban standing extra slots; yet the Japan review showed the family *is* this system's class community with no community mechanism: no rota assignment point for the J-1 contribution ladder, no group reflection (*hansei* exists only as project debrief and solo review), and nowhere for a quest's "what changes next time?" to land.

**Boss milestones (era boundaries).** All five national systems converge on exactly two: **fluent +/− within 20 at ~7** (Foundations→Builder gate; completable during the koolieelik year *or* grade 1 — no deadline, CR14) and **one-digit ×/÷ fluency at ~9** (Explorer→Voyager gate). **Semantics (binding, for poster and frontier tooling):** a boss milestone is (a) a **ceremonial era marker** — its consolidation triggers the era-completion ceremony (parent signs, poster celebrated) — and (b) an **ordinary hard prerequisite inside its own numeracy chain**. It is **never a cross-domain gate at branch level — a scoped rule:** within **tiers 0–3**, no literacy, science, logic, SEL or other non-numeracy *branch* node is ever locked behind a boss (GP1 domain-specificity). **Career quest nodes and tier-4/5 milestone bundles are explicitly carved out** of this rule — the same carve-out the bridge-cap definition carries (§6): they sit above the domains and legitimately inherit the numeracy chain (change-making needs within-20 arithmetic; a venture needs money sense), so a boss may sit in their ancestry without violating anything. Tooling that enforces this rule must scope the check to tier-0–3 branch nodes. And the next era's poster goes up on the wall when the child's frontier first crosses into that tier in *any* branch — the ceremony celebrates, it withholds nothing. Use the Estonian grade-3 outcome list (numbers 0–10,000, full korrutustabel, fractions 1/2–1/5, perimeter, 2-step problems; fluent comprehending reading) as the compact **~9–10** checkpoint (end of grade 3, CR14) — it is official and the family's legal reference point; the mastery ladder may meet it up to a year early, and is never pushed to. [R3 §3.2 convergence note, §6.1, §2.1; CR14]

**Daily time budget (binding).** The direct-instruction dose sits **inside** the session, never on top of it: the session row above is the *total* daily system time. At 5–6 the 10–15-min session is guided play in register, with at most ~10 min of it explicitly structured on training-ground days (CR3's "scheduled dose, never the whole diet"). From 6–7 onward the session alternates emphasis — phonics-led vs. number-led days — rather than stacking both explicit doses daily. Everything outside the session is ordinary childhood: free play and life — plus exactly **one named exception, the family read-aloud rail (CR18)**: ~10 minutes daily, Estonian and English alternating or stacked, priced in §5.6 — the delivery vehicle of the `lit-en-oral-*` oral leg and the soil of Estonian reading appetite, sanctioned by rule rather than smuggled as goodwill. The weekly template below fixes which sessions carry which dose. [CR3; CR18; GP11; R1 §7.4]

**Weekly session template (binding).** The daily budget only closes if the week is partitioned — the dose, the B18 booklet spread and the retrieval strip all fit because they never all land in the same session. Out of ~6 weekly sessions (Bands 1–2) or ~5 (Bands 3–4):

- **Band 1 (5–6):** every session is guided play chosen from the surfaced frontier; **~2 sessions/week** carry the ≤10-min structured dose (one sound-play day, one number-play day). Booklet spreads happen whenever the child picks the booklet from the frontier — no fixed slot.
- **Band 2 (6–7):** **2 phonics-led** sessions (the ~10–12-min explicit dose inside, CR3's ramp — each carrying a standing **invited-echo line** since v1.7 (SGP S-9): the child *may* say the sounds and words back, invited, never required, never checked), **2 number-led** sessions (the ~10-min retrieval dose inside — **pull-conditional since v1.7, CR14/FIN-9:** these two slots run only while the child is pulling number work; absent pull they revert to booklet/free-choice sessions, the frontier still surfacing number roots), **2 booklet/free-choice** sessions (one working spread each, B18). The letter-formation dose — the third training-ground strand at this band — lives in the booklet sessions' form-drawing and letter-lane warm-up pages, not in a separate slot.
- **Band 3 (7–8):** 2 phonics→spelling sessions, 1 number-fact session, 2 booklet/free-choice sessions (school now carries part of the load).
- **Band 4 (8–9):** 1 written-expression session, 1–2 fluency-maintenance sessions, 2 booklet/project sessions.

The **family read-aloud rail (CR18)** runs daily alongside every band's template — outside the sessions, the one named daily item, carrying reading and talk only (checks stay inside sessions) — and the **weekly family meeting (CR20, v1.7)** runs alongside it once a week: ~10 minutes, the second and last named exemption — jobs rotate, everyone reflects, the child chairs from ~7. Every session, whatever its flavor, opens with the ~2-minute retrieval strip (B18); the strips carry maintenance of whichever strands are *not* that day's emphasis — that is what lets the doses alternate without decay. Unlock trials replace the free-choice tail of at most one session on trial weeks. **Autonomy inside dose sessions (ruled, v1.6):** bounded choice must not shrink with age against the SDT gradient — within any dose or fluency session the child picks the order and the format among equivalent materials (B11's buffet logic extended upward: which table first, cards or board, which decodable), so Bands 3–4 keep real choice inside their prescribed slots. [R2 §9.4] The eight non-training-ground domains are not squeezed into leftover minutes: they **are** the booklet/free-choice sessions, reached through the booklet line's domain rotation and the 3–5-node frontier the child chooses from (§5.1). [CR3; CR16; GP8; B18]

**Node cadences are subordinate to this template (binding).** The session row is the *whole* weekly child budget (§5.6 prices it), so node-level exercise prescriptions can never add time on top: **every exercise-cadence line in the learning path must name the template slot that carries it** — the retrieval strip, a dose session, a fluency-maintenance session, a booklet/project session, or the CR18 read-aloud rail. "Daily" prescriptions do not exist outside the ~2-minute strips and the named read-aloud rail (CR18 — the one exemption, by name; non-session days carry no other system work, by rule). Two guard-rails make this auditable: (a) **at most two training-ground nodes whose dose occupies a template session slot may sit in Practicing concurrently** — a strand whose minutes ride the booklet sessions' warm-up pages rather than a slot of its own (letter formation at Band 2; handwriting's weekly copy passage at Bands 3–4) or that is school-evidenced (`lit-reading-fluency`, §7 sourcing note of the learning path) does **not** count against the cap, so the band templates' three named concurrent strands are the cap working as designed, never an exception to it; the frontier curation rule §5.1 already gives the parent the lever; (b) any tier-2/3 node whose exercise line implies minutes is **audited against its band's session budget before authoring ships** (loop 3 audit: the times-tables minutes now ride the fluency-maintenance sessions and the strips; typing rides the booklet/project sessions — see the node cards). [CR3; CR16; §5.6]

**Scheduling texture within bands:** daily rhythm alternates focused and expansive activity; series-level planning uses block-and-revisit waves; an optional light seasonal layer (autumn = measuring/harvest, winter = story/reading) gives families a natural scheduler — extended since v1.6 to the **Estonian folk calendar** (mardipäev, kadripäev, jõulud, vastlapäev, with their songs joining `cre-music-play`'s repertoire), the scheduled home of cultural belonging under CR17. **Since v1.7 the seasonal layer carries three real vehicles, so its scheduling claim ships content rather than decoration (FIN-11; JPN J-6/J-8):** *(a)* the **seasonal phenomenon week** — 2–4 times a year, the two booklet/free-choice sessions of one week go to **one child-chosen question about the world** (*Why is it dark so long? Where does bread come from? What happens to our trash?*), pulled across ≥3 domains, documented on B19 surfaces — the one integration format that starts from the child's question rather than a domain column, three years before the tier-3 quests; a phenomenon that catches fire becomes a claimed wild node (CR6, with §5.1's frontier slot carrying it), and the folk-calendar feasts are natural hosts (mardipäev *is* a phenomenon week waiting to be run); *(b)* the **quest-revival tradition** (§5.1, v1.7) — the seasonal layer is the scheduler for voluntary re-runs of completed quests at bigger scale, the previous run's debrief sheet read aloud first; *(c)* the **four-season weather strip** — the tier-0 week-long strip returns one week per season, same strip, compared against last season's (spaced retrieval wearing seasonal clothes), and `sci-body-health`'s care-for-a-living-thing log keys to spring planting where the family can. ## 4. Exercise & booklet design guidelines

Unified rules (B#), merging R1's twelve booklet rules, R2's 24-row evidence table, R3 §6.2 and R4 C5. Each rule is binding for every booklet.

### 4.1 Layout & instruction design

**B1 — Page anatomy.** Working area visually clean and Montessori-spare; instructions live *inside/next to* the grid as a solved example row, never as prose above it; parent-facing text in a small footer, separated; decoration quarantined to covers, transition and reward pages. [R1 BR6, §2.4; R2 r2, r5 (split-attention, redundancy)]

**B2 — Zero-reading instructions at 5–7.** A consistent icon set + one modeled example carry every instruction (the "prepared environment on paper"); any English sentence is addressed to the parent to read aloud. The same iconography spans the entire system. **Register extension (v1.7, SGP S-9):** any English the child *volunteers* — an echoed sound, a word, a whole sentence — is received warmly and never corrected mid-flow; production is invited by the environment and demanded by nothing. [R1 BR7; R2 r19; CR18]

**B3 — Grid module by age.** 2 cm cells at 5, 1.5 cm at 7, 1 cm at 9 — constant across all booklet families so difficulty lives in the task, never in the paper. Motor demand ramps: place cut-out tiles → circle/color → draw glyphs → write symbols. [R1 §5.4, BR3, BR8; R3 §3.5]

**B4 — Externalize memory onto the page.** Symbol banks printed at top, scratch boxes, candidate-mark corners, picture checklists — the sheet holds the state so the head doesn't have to. [R2 r12, §3.2]

**B5 — Thin narrative wrapper.** Recurring cast frames the booklet; the story lives between exercises and on covers, never inside the working area (CR7). [R1 BR6; R2 r5]

**B6 — Hand-made aesthetic.** Warm, watercolor-ish, hand-drawn frames; calm and beautiful, not clip-art — a deliberate market differentiator and a Waldorf/Montessori convergence. [R1 §3.4, §2.4]

### 4.2 Difficulty ramping

**B7 — One new difficulty per page; one new mechanic per booklet.** Change grid size OR symbol set OR rule — never two at once. [R1 BR2; R2 r6; R4 A1]

**B8 — Grade difficulty by inference depth, not surface size.** Sudoku levels are defined by required deduction chain (scan → single elimination → two-step), not just grid dimensions; a 4×4 needing a two-step inference outranks a 6×6 needing single-step scans. [R2 r6 (element interactivity)]

**B9 — CPA ladder inside every mechanic.** Cut-out tiles to place → pictures to draw → line-art glyphs → digits/letters (Froebel's solid→surface→line→point as the explicit art-style dial). Ship the tile sheet in booklet 1. Bridge pages show the *identical* puzzle in pictures and in symbols side-by-side to prove equivalence. [R1 BR3, §5.4, §8.1 S4; R2 r13; R3 §6.2]

**B10 — Explore → example → practice → create, in order.** Per new concept: (1) exploration page with the materials; (2) ONE worked example showing rules, not strategies (strategy discovery stays with the child until ~level 5, where one named strategy may be taught); (3) recognition tasks before production ~3:1 on new-concept introduction pages (scope ruling CR15 — self-checking production sets are exempt from the ratio), with fading support (completion problems: partially solved grids, shrinking symbol banks, hints going to zero by set's end — training wheels must actually come off); (4) "make your own" finale (tip: "start from a solved grid and erase"). [R1 BR4, §8.1; R2 r3–r4 (worked example, completion, expertise reversal); R1 §7.3 (Bonawitz)]

**B11 — Parallel-difficulty buffet.** ≥3 same-level puzzles before each step up; the child chooses order. Never one-of-each-difficulty escalators. [R1 BR11; R2 r16]

**B12 — Flow-channel calibration.** ~80% of each booklet solvable comfortably; final ~2 pages stretch. If the child's success drops below ~70%, provide a *different-representation* corrective one rung down, not the same page again. [R4 C5, A1; R2 r11]

**B13 — Life / Beauty / Knowledge page rotation.** Every mechanic appears three ways: a real-world scene (set the table so every plate gets one of each = sudoku logic), a decorative/pattern variant (symmetric tile design — free but constrained), and the formal puzzle. This is the honest way to triple booklet length and the paper version of multiple representations (GP14). **Two standing Life-page assignments join the rotation in v1.7:** *(a)* **each printed booklet keys one Life page to its season of print** (vastlapäev sled-distance measuring, mardipäev mask craft, jõulud baking-by-recipe) — the folk-calendar layer's shipped vehicle, so CR17's "scheduled home of cultural belonging" is true on paper, not only in prose (JPN J-8); *(b)* a **waste-sort / repair-don't-replace Life page** sits in the rotation's cycle (sort the family recycling; fix the toy before replacing it) — the sustainability lens's cheapest carrier (§6, FIN-13). Both are rotation content, not new page budget. [R1 BR5, §5.4; R2 r24; CR17; §6 lenses]

**B14 — Small sets, child-sensible framing at 5–6.** Quantities ≤5 then ≤10 at entry; tasks framed with animals/food/play the child recognizes, not abstract prompts. [R2 r14 (Gelman; Donaldson); R3 §5 depth-over-range]

### 4.3 Feedback & self-correction

**B15 — Every page self-checks.** Unique-solution puzzles by preference (the child *sees* the clash of two identical pictures in a row); otherwise fold-over answer flaps, count-the-dots verifications, or checksum pictures that only assemble when answers are right. The adult is never grader. [R1 BR1; R2 r8]

**B16 — Recognition-of-error pages.** Every new size/mechanic includes one "find the mistake in this finished grid" page before production pages — error-spotting doubles as debugging (computational thinking) and as the Waldorf whole-before-parts inversion. [R1 §8.1, §3.4; R3 §6.2 ("find the mistake" = debugging)]

**B17 — Scripted parent behavior in footers.** Per hard page: an "if stuck, say this" contingent hint line, and a process-praise prompt ("notice aloud the strategy they used" + sample sentence — praise the process that worked, not "smart"). Mixed-review pages carry the note that feeling slower is the point. **Three script extensions (v1.7):** form-drawing warm-up footers carry one line on **posture and unhurried strokes** — care, not speed, is the warm-up's register (JPN J-7); once a week the parent **narrates one own mistake and its fix aloud** ("I planned the shop trip badly — next time I'll write the list first"), with the CR20 meeting's parent turn as its scheduled home — children believe reflection when adults model it (JPN J-9); and the **identity-talk script** (JPN J-10): when the child says "I'm going to be a doctor" or "I'm bad at the engineer path," **praise the pull, never affirm or deny the label** — "you love finding out how bodies work," not "our little doctor" — the Mueller & Dweck process-praise rule extended to identity labels. [R2 §2 (Wood/Bruner/Ross), r17–r18 (Mueller & Dweck; Gunderson); R1 §7.3; CR20; JPN J-7/J-9/J-10]

**B18 — Retrieval warm-up + built-in spacing/interleaving (cadence pinned, arithmetic closed).** A booklet is **20–28 pages, of which ~16–20 are working pages (8–10 two-page working spreads)** — covers, story/transition, reward and documentation pages consume no sessions. (Loop 3 trimmed the earlier 10–13-spread sketch: the §3 template grants **exactly 2** booklet/free-choice sessions per week in every band, so 8–10 spreads is what actually makes the equivalence below true — B13's Life/Beauty/Knowledge rotation still supplies the variety inside fewer spreads.) **One working spread per *booklet session*** — the 2 booklet/free-choice sessions of the §3 weekly template, *not* every session — so a booklet spans **≈4–5 weeks ≈ one month**; this booklet≈month equivalence is what the N−1 and N−3/N−4 gap labels below rest on. **Band-1 exception, stated:** Band-1 spreads have no fixed slot (§3 template), so at Band 1 the gap labels key to **elapsed time, not booklet indices** — "last week's pages," "about a month ago," "about a season ago" — whatever booklet they came from. Retrieval runs **per session, not per booklet**: every session — dose day or booklet day — opens with a 4-item retrieval strip (~2 min, immediate self-check, GP8) sampling four gap rungs: (a) earlier pages of the *same* spread's week (**days-scale**), (a′) the *previous week's* pages (**≈1 week** — GP8's week rung, previously unrepresented), (b) booklet N−1 (**≈1 month** back), and (c) booklet N−3/N−4 (**≈1 season** back) — expanding gaps under honest labels. A single strip samples one or two rungs, never all four; the rotation covers all four across the week, and §5.1 rules priority when consolidation retests and Needs-polish refresh compete for strip items. Each new skill gets one blocked page then appears mixed with confusable older skills (5+3 beside 5−3; b beside d) in that booklet and all later ones. Blocked practice dominates at 5–6; interleaving ramps at 7+. Consolidation retests (§5.1) ride these strips under the §5.1 ruled mechanism — no separate retest sessions exist. [R2 r8–r10; R1 BR9; GP8]

**B19 — Documentation surface.** Start page: date + self-portrait + "I can already…" checkboxes. Margin: "my thinking" line (parent scribes at 5–6). One provocation page per booklet (e.g., "this grid has TWO answers — find both; which do you like better?"). End page: link to the wall tree (stamp/sticker instructions) — so a shelf of finished booklets is a visible growth record. [R1 BR10, §4.4; R4 A5 (evidence log)]

**B20 — No per-page extrinsic rewards.** Completion is marked by capability statements ("You can now solve 6×6!") and tree progress — never stars/points per exercise, and never per-sitting tokens for time-on-task (persistence included: the reward for staying with a puzzle is the capability statement at node completion, not a stamp per sitting). [R1 BR12; R2 r15; R4 A5]

**B21 — Cross-language contrast pages.** Estonian and English share an alphabet but not letter–sound values; R2 §10.2's flagged interference points get an explicit design element: clashing GPCs (Estonian *i* vs. English /ɪ/–/aɪ/; *j*; *u*; *o*; Estonian vowel-doubling for length vs. English vowel teams) are never introduced in the same week; each clash gets a side-by-side contrast page ("in Estonian this letter says…; in English it says…") anchored by language-flag icons; early decodables avoid Estonian–English false-friend spellings. [R2 §10.2; GP10]

**B22 — Audio models & parent pronunciation micro-guides (SSP delivery; v1.6).** The English decoding strand is delivered 1:1 by an Estonian-speaking parent, and an SSP lives or dies on pure GPC pronunciation — /m/ not "muh", English short vowels Estonian does not have; a parent who models /æ/ as Estonian *ä* for two years bakes in errors no school will later fix (school English starts at grade 3 and won't do phonics). Therefore, binding for every phonics booklet: **each new GPC gets an audio model** (QR-linked per-GPC audio, or supplied by a named program-with-audio the family follows — printable-plus-audio is a solved problem), and **each new GPC set gets a parent pronunciation micro-guide** (say-it-like-this lines, the no-added-schwa rule, explicit contrast against the nearest Estonian value on B21's pattern). B17's scripted footers extend to phonics pages accordingly. This rule is the booklet-level half of §5.4's parent-delivery ruling. [R2 §10.2; GP10; B17; B21; §5.4]

**B23 — Every practice set is a designed variation set (v1.7).** Within-set procedural variation is a design rule, not a hope: for each practice set the author states **what varies and what stays constant**, varying one dimension at a time while the deep structure holds (7+2, 7+3, 7+4 — *what changed, and what did it do to the answer?*); contrast sets for confusable structures are built as **adjacent items**, not merely interleaved pages (8 = 5 + □ beside □ + 3 = 8 beside 8 − 5 = □ as one designed set). B7 isolates difficulties (restriction) and B11 supplies parallel repetition (volume); B23 governs the one thing neither touched — the *internal order of the item set*, which in the Singapore tradition is itself the lesson: an author following every prior B-rule to the letter could still ship ten unrelated correct items. [R3 §2.5 (Dienes systematic variation); SGP S-4; GP6; B7; B11]

**B24 — Non-routine puzzler pages and the multi-day Big Problem (v1.7 — Singapore S-2 and Japan J-4, merged into one culture).** Two page classes teach the child to *choose an attack* and to *hold a hard problem*: *(a)* **puzzler pages** — every numeracy booklet from tier 2 carries one page of 2–3 **non-routine problems**: the method is not given, more than one legitimate attack exists (draw a picture, guess-and-check, make a systematic list, work backwards), and heuristics are **named only after they are met** (the S5 idiom — self-found first, named later); the parent footer says slower is the point (B17). *(b)* **the Big Problem page** — from Band 2, every booklet carries **one marked multi-day problem** expected *not* to fall in one sitting (one inference step past the band's B8 ladder), visually marked as multi-day, whose footer script **forbids hints and replaces them**: *"What did you try? What do we know now? Where shall we leave it for tomorrow?"* — returning tomorrow is the designed outcome, never a failure path, and GP17's drop-a-rung corrective is explicitly scoped away from it (GP17 v1.7 clause: checks and practice sets only). Each era's remembered *takai mondai* — "the problem that took me four days" — may attach to keystone evidence as the child's own trophy of held struggle. In a numeracy booklet the Big Problem may simply be the hardest puzzler; the two classes are one culture, not two page taxes. [SGP S-2; JPN J-4; GP17; B8; B12 (the ~80% comfort budget already reserves stretch room); B17]

**B25 — Compare-two-ways pages (v1.7).** Beyond B15's private self-correction and B16's find-the-mistake, every booklet family adds a **compare-two-ways page class** to B10's battery: two worked solutions to the same problem, side by side — sometimes one flawed, sometimes both valid but one more elegant — with the prompts *"Who is right? Which way do you like better? Say why."* Run as talk (the COM because-bridge is the vehicle), with the parent scripted to **defend the losing method for one round** so the child must argue, not just pick. This is the public half of error culture the system previously lacked: control of error (GP4) teaches self-correction; compare-two-ways teaches the *weighing of reasoning* — the neriage move, and the muscle the lawyer, scientist and manager profiles all claim. (B19's two-solutions provocation stays its cousin: that page compares two *answers* to one puzzle; B25 compares two *ways* to one answer.) [JPN J-5; GP4; R2 r3 (worked examples, extended to contrasting cases); B16; B19]

## 5. Progress & mastery assessment approach

### 5.1 The mastery state machine

Mastery is a state, not a checkbox [R4 A6.7, C3.3]:

**Locked → Available (outer fringe) → Practicing → Mastered → Consolidated → Needs-polish**

- **Available** = all hard prerequisites are *Consolidated*; the child's visible frontier is **3–5** Available nodes (the single canonical figure — GP9 now matches), child picks (ALEKS outer-fringe discipline + SDT autonomy). **Wild-node frontier slot (v1.7, FIN-12 — the valve wired to the mechanism that actually governs the week):** once a child has claimed a wild node (CR6), **one surfaced frontier slot always carries it** (or another child-claimed item) until the child retires it — the ~10–15% content-agency share becomes a floor with a delivery mechanism, not a ceiling with a hope; under schedule pressure, curation may never squeeze the claimed interest to zero. **Deep or forward? (v1.7, SGP S-6):** weekly frontier curation explicitly owns the depth-vs-advance choice — for a child cruising above ~95% on a strand, GP17's ceiling response (the stretch variant, B24's non-routine sets) is surfaced *before* the next rung. [R4 A3, C3.6; R2 r16; CR6; GP17]
- **Starting-frontier rule (day 1).** All tier-0 roots are technically Available on day one; the poster surfaces exactly **4** to begin — one numeracy root, one literacy root, one from logic/digital/spatial, and one the child picks from the rest **or anything the child proposes** (the Reggio clause, v1.7: a proposal becomes a claimed wild node on the spot, CR6) — and the parent rotates the surfaced set weekly until natural unlocks take over. The child always chooses *among* the surfaced 3–5; the parent curates *which* are surfaced. [GP9; R4 A3; CR6]
- **Mastered** = passed the node's mastery check: performed independently, self-checked. [R1 TR1; R2 r1]
- **Consolidated** = check passed again ≥2 weeks later (spaced verification à la Khan Mastery Challenges); the retest rides the B18 warm-up strips, never a separate session — **and what a strip-borne retest *is* is ruled, not implied:** a consolidation retest = **3–4 items drawn from the node's check bank, spread across two consecutive session strips in the retest week** (the week ≥2 weeks after Mastered; typically 2 items per strip). All items correct → Consolidated. Any error → the node stays Mastered, its content re-enters ordinary strip sampling, and the retest re-queues ~2 weeks later. This is **R4 A6.7's two-pass model with the second pass distributed into strips**, counted in *check events*: two verification stages — the §5.2 mastery check (one check event, itself run in two sittings ≥3 days apart per Archetype A), then the strip-borne retest; no further check event exists, and neither stage may be collapsed to fewer sittings than its own spec names. **The weekly arithmetic, done once:** 5–6 sessions × 4 strip items ≈ **20–24 strip items/week** against ~5 active strands; a single strip never carries items from more than two strands. **Strip sampling priority:** (1) retest items for nodes in their retest week — at most **2 nodes queued per week** (the placement sweep and era boundaries batch the rest; two queued nodes take different days' strips); (2) Needs-polish refresh items (§5.6 dim-check output); (3) the B18 gap-rung maintenance mix (days/week/N−1/season), rotating strands across the week rather than cramming each strip. A week's strips must cover every priority-1 and priority-2 obligation before maintenance breadth. Only Consolidated satisfies prerequisites for downstream nodes — with the tier-0 exception below. **Consolidation by node type (ruled once, for tooling):** Archetype-A performance checks consolidate by the standard ≥2-week strip-borne re-pass above; Archetype-B observation tallies consolidate on **one further logged instance ≥2 weeks after mastery** (not a second full tally); **quest nodes and month-scale dispositional nodes** (goal charts, savings jars — flagged, accepted deviations from the 1–3-week granularity rule, §5.2) are **single-shot**: Consolidated = Mastered at completion, because the activity is itself weeks long and self-spacing — nobody re-runs restaurant night for a checkbox. **And yet the events themselves may return — the quest-revival tradition (v1.7, JPN J-6):** any completed quest may be *voluntarily* re-run in a later season or year at bigger scale (restaurant night for the grandparents; the market stall at a real school fair; family court writing a rule-of-the-house that stands), **the previous run's debrief sheet read aloud first** — the event becomes a spiral and the child meets their own growth against a fixed format, which is what the quest cards' own "what changes next time?" lines were always pointing at. Revivals live at the poster/seasonal layer (§3) and are planned at the CR20 meeting, **never in the graph**: tree state is untouched, Consolidated stays Consolidated, and no revival is ever required or tracked. **Cluster consolidation (ruled):** in a cluster node, each named sub-check consolidates under its *own* archetype (A: strip-borne re-pass; B: one further logged instance; a weeks-long chart sub-check: single-shot at its completion); the node is Consolidated when all its sub-checks are. **Machine-readable:** every node in the learning-path graph carries an optional `check` field — `"A"` (the default when absent), `"B"`, `"single-shot"`, or `"mixed"` (clusters whose sub-checks differ, with each sub-check's archetype named in the node description) — so tooling never re-derives archetypes from prose. [R4 C3.3, A2, A6.7; R2 r9]
- **Tier-0 exception (binding).** Within tier 0, intra-tier prerequisite edges are satisfied at **Mastered**, not Consolidated. The entry band must never carry a hidden multi-week consolidation tax: a five-year-old who just mastered sorting should open the next puzzle that week, not in a month (review found `num-bonds-5`, `lit-letter-knowledge`, `dig-robot-game` and booklet #1's home node all silently locked behind ~4–6 weeks of ceremony under the unscoped rule). Consolidation still happens — retests ride the warm-up strips as usual — it just never gates a tier-0 neighbor. From tier 1 upward the Consolidated rule applies unchanged.
- **Needs-polish** = decay timer expired (8–12 weeks without use for fluency skills); the node visibly "dims" on the poster and its content re-enters booklet warm-ups until refreshed. A node mastered at 6 is not assumed mastered at 8. **A Needs-polish node continues to satisfy prerequisites**: downstream subtrees never re-lock and the poster never un-colors — a summer holiday may dim the map, it must not darken it (§5.5); refresh happens through warm-ups, not re-gating. [R4 A1 (Ebbinghaus row), C3.3; R2 §6 design implications]

**Placement ruling (cold start).** A child does not begin at "day 1 of tier 0" unless that is truly their frontier. On entry — a 6–8-year-old joining the system, or a 5-year-old arriving with tier-0 skills already in hand — the parent runs a **batched placement sweep**: the same per-node checks, several per sitting in dependency order, spread over at most one to two weeks. Nodes passed at placement are marked **Consolidated directly** (the ≥2-week re-pass is waived for placement only — it certifies prior, already-spaced learning, not fresh mastery), and their decay timers start at the placement date. The sweep stops at the first genuine frontier; already-possessed skills get no unlock-ceremony queue — weeks of ceremony before reaching real work is exactly the demotivation pattern §5.5 bans. [GP1; §5.5]

### 5.2 Mastery criteria

Every node ships with a written, parent-runnable criterion [R2 r1; R4 C2]:

- Format: can-do statement + check spec — e.g., "adds within 10 promptly and without counting — the parent listens for retrieval or a fast derived fact — 9/10 correct, two sessions ≥3 days apart" (the two-sessions clause bakes in spacing; the **retrieval-listen replaced the earlier numeric latency bar in v1.7, FIN-16** — a parent cannot apply a seconds criterion without timing, a hidden stopwatch is still a stopwatch, and the qualitative listen for strategy-free answers carries the whole automaticity load, R2 §8's construct being strategy, not milliseconds). [R2 §8; §5.5]
- Granularity: masterable in 1–3 weeks of 10–20-minute sessions; checkable with 5–10 items in <10 minutes. "Knows addition" is too big; "adds 3+4" is too small; "adds two numbers within 20 crossing ten" is right. [R4 C2]
- **Two check archetypes.** *Archetype A — performance check* (the default, and everything above): 5–10 items, parent-runnable in <10 minutes, passed twice ≥3 days apart. *Archetype B — observation tally* (dispositional SEL/EF nodes: persistence, cooperation, conflict resolution, team roles, goal-setting): a concrete naturalistic threshold — **≥3 unprompted instances logged across ≥2 weeks** on the node's evidence line — because dispositions are observed in life, not examined at a table. Every node is tagged with its archetype in the booklet pipeline; Archetype-B consolidation satisfies prerequisite edges exactly like Archetype-A. **Tally validity guard-rails (v1.6 — the rater is a parent who wants the poster to advance, so the tallies get rigor, not trust):** each logged instance is a **one-line described observation** (what happened, when — description resists inflation better than a tick); a keystone-level Archetype-B node needs at least one instance countersigned by a second adult or drawn from outside the family context; and **second-hand evidence from a named non-family adult is explicitly legitimate** — for peer-facing skills (conflict resolution, team roles) it is *expected*, since the behavior happens at lasteaed, school or huviring where the parent mostly is not: a kindergarten teacher's comment or a coach's word counts as an instance, logged with the source. **Rota-reliability semantics for owned-job sub-checks (v1.7, JPN J-2):** where a sub-check is an owned family job (`sel-selfcare`(c), the `sel-contribution` ladder), the tally unit is a **completed rota cycle — the job done all week with ≤1 reminder — not an unprompted instance**: "unprompted" measures spontaneity, and duty's virtue is *dependability under the rota*, including on the days the child doesn't feel like it (under the old semantics a child who spontaneously helped three times and shirked the rest would consolidate the node while the reliably-serving child tallied nothing). This also names a tension the framework had left implicit: motivation safety selects pleasant, chosen tasks, while duty-formation needs some required, unglamorous ones — the rota threads that needle without rewards or nagging, and it costs GP9 nothing because nothing is paid and nothing gates. [R4 C2, A5 (the scouting model's non-parent verifier); R2 §8; GP16; GP9]
- **Cluster nodes (bounded exception to "one can-do").** A named handful of nodes bundle 2–4 *same-domain sibling* can-dos that share materials into one poster cell (marked `cluster: true` in the graph data). Each named sub-skill keeps its own <10-minute check; the node is Mastered only when **all** sub-checks pass; partial passes show as pencil ticks on the cell. Bundling is never allowed across domains or to hide load. [R4 C2, amended]
- **Extended-fluency nodes (bounded exception to "1–3 weeks").** Fluency strands that genuinely take months (English phonics phases, reading fluency, handwriting automatization) exceed the 1–3-week window by nature. They are flagged as training grounds (CR1, `strand: "training-ground"`), carry **internal sub-level ticks** (e.g. GPC sets; table sub-sets 2·5·10 → 3·4 inside the first-tables node, then 6·7 → 8·9 inside the full-tables node) so the poster shows motion at least monthly (GP13 anti-stall), and the two year-scale numeracy fluencies are additionally *split into staged graph nodes* (within-10 → within-20; first-tables → full-tables) so no child sits in "Practicing" for six months without a tree event. [R4 C2, amended; GP13]
- **Tier-4 bundles and the decomposition trigger (ruled, v1.6).** Tier-4/5 milestone bundles are bounded exceptions to granularity by design (learning-path §1) — but "decomposed when the family approaches them" had no owner, no trigger and no date, leaving multi-year bundles with no tick mechanism exactly at ages ~8–10 where R2 §9.4 says self-assessment turns accurate and motivation turns fragile. Ruled: **when the child's frontier first enters tier 4 in any branch, that branch's bundles are decomposed into granular nodes within one quarter** (owner: the next revision of the learning path, triggered by the family's own quarterly dim-check); until decomposition, **every tier-4 bundle carries interim sub-level ticks** (the extended-fluency tick mechanism extended upward — reading levels climbed, projects shipped — authored, since learning-path v1.8, as each bundle's `rungs` ladder in the learning path's canonical JSON, which the app and the poster pipeline consume rather than author; the tier-5 vestibules carry authored `rungs` on the same mechanism, and rungs are ticks only, never graph nodes and never a substitute for this trigger) so GP13's monthly poster motion survives the coarse layer. **`t4-english-fluent` decomposes first wherever the trigger fires**: it is the system's steepest un-staged middle, carries the flagship strand, and must gain the **productive-spoken-English rung** no earlier node supplies (every oral node to tier 3 is deliberately receptive), alongside staged graded-reading levels and English composition — carrying over the card's own concession that cross-language writing transfer holds for structure and not for spelling. [GP13; R2 §9.4; learning-path §1]
- **Check administration protocol (Archetype A, ruled, v1.6 — age-scoped in v1.7).** The parent is asked to make live judgments ("retrieval or finger-counting?") with no training, so the run itself is scripted: neutral face and voice; **no teaching and no hints mid-check** (hint-supported work is practice, not a check — B17's hint lines apply to practice pages only); the first error is information, never a prompt to coach; after 2 misses, stop warmly ("we'll come back to this one") and return ≥3 days later **with the different-representation corrective first** (GP17); no same-day retries. **This protocol applies from Band 2** — see the Band-1 register below. [R2 §8; GP17; B17]
- **Band-1 check register (ruled, v1.7, FIN-6 — the check machinery scoped by age).** The neutral-face protocol's validity rationale is real for a 6–7-year-old entering formal strands; at 5, a silent, deliberately non-helping parent **is test aesthetics in the child's phenomenology** — the child reads the professionally blank face and learns *this moment is different; I am being measured* — so CR11's ban was being honored materially and violated socially. Therefore, at Band 1 (tier 0), Archetype-A checks are **observed or played, never administered as sittings**: *(a)* a tier-0 A-check may be evidenced by **accumulated observation of the criterion behavior in ordinary play across ordinary days** (logged like Archetype-B instances — the child counts 17 buttons with cardinality at the kitchen table, the parent logs it), **or** run as a **game the child does not experience as a sitting** — the 1b unlock trial (two fresh puzzle grids ≥3 days apart, solved alone, self-checked, no scores, no timer) is the **named template**, and most tier-0 A-checks are visible in normal play or in the booklet pages themselves; *(b)* the neutral-face administration protocol above applies **from Band 2**; *(c)* Band-1 retrieval strips are **explicitly game-format** (icon-and-tile, B2) — stated once so booklet authors cannot drift. Nothing about the *criteria* changes — only the register of verification, which is precisely what GP11 says the band is about; this is how esiopetus certifies the same skills, and the system's own 1b trial proves the standard is reachable in-house. [FIN-6; GP11; CR11; B2; §4.4]
- **Language of the check (ruled, v1.6).** Any check outside the LIT-EN strand may be administered **in either language, with instructions read aloud on request, at every age**. B2 protects 5–7 via icons; from Band 4 the materials assume short independent English instructions, and without this ruling every non-language check from ~8 silently conflates the target skill with English proficiency — a math check failed for vocabulary reasons is a validity failure, so the read-aloud-on-request protection never lapses. [B2; R2 §8; GP10]
- Failing a check triggers a *different* corrective exercise (different representation, one rung down), never the same page again (Guskey; ZPD). [R2 r11; R4 A1]
- Checks are framed as **unlock trials**, tiny and low-stakes; no displayed scores, no comparisons, no test aesthetics (CR11). [R2 §8; R3 §5 assessment row]

### 5.3 Evidence & documentation (assessment = the visible record)

- **Evidence log per node** (Open Badges' load-bearing idea): "mastered 2026-09-14, booklet 3, retest 2026-09-28" — kept simple, e.g., in the node data model (`R4 C6` YAML: `mastery_check`, `consolidation`, `decay_weeks` fields). [R4 A5, C6]
- **The wall poster is the assessment artifact**: child colors/stamps completed nodes, parent signs (ceremony over prizes — relatedness), a work sample or dictated sentence attaches to keystone nodes ("what I can do now / what surprised me"). Making learning visible *is* the assessment. **The fair copy (v1.7, JPN J-7 — the care-of-work ritual):** the keystone work sample is made as a **deliberate best version for the attachment** — posture, unhurried strokes, the child's own standard — not pulled from the pile; the grandparent letter in `lit-composition` is the canonical first instance (a fair copy *because someone will receive it* — care as service). Adults never grade or rank a fair copy; the child's own eye for quality is the thing being trained, and the monthly child-chosen "most beautiful page" on `mot-handwriting-auto` trains the same muscle. This is quality-of-work's one mechanism in the system — GP9-safe because the child chooses and nobody scores. [R1 TR5, §4.5, §3.4 (Waldorf main-lesson book); R4 A5; JPN J-7; GP9]
- **Booklet documentation surfaces** (B19) feed the same record: the finished-booklet shelf is the longitudinal portfolio. [R1 BR10]
- **Dynamic, not static, difficulty probing**: the parent hint-line protocol (B17) makes every hard page a lightweight ZPD probe — what the child does with one hint tells you the frontier. [R2 §2]

### 5.4 External anchors

- **Age-6/7:** the Estonian *koolivalmidus* (school-readiness) profile — stated honestly since v1.6 (the earlier "demonstrably meet" claim outran the graph): the tree's tier-0/1 coverage **demonstrably covers the language, math, motor, social, self-care, safety and time-orientation criteria** (letters + sounding out 1–2-syllable words, counting to 12, +/− within 5, shapes, pencil/scissors, social criteria; since the v1.6 life stratum also independence in dressing/eating, tidying, pedestrian traffic rules, days of the week, and rhymes/poems by heart via `cre-music-play`), and **names lasteaed as the delivering source for the rest** — Estonia-and-its-symbols, group music-making, gymnastics/skiing/skating norms (home-side watch only, `mot-gross-2`) — per CR17's division of labor. The tree *plus a normal lasteaed year* double as school-readiness evidence; the tree alone does not claim to. [R3 §2.1; R4 B4; CR17]
- **Age ~9–10:** the Estonian grade-3 outcome list as compact checkpoint (end of grade 3, CR14 — the mastery ladder may meet it up to a year early, never demanded early); the two consensus boss milestones (+/− within 20 fluent at ~7, completable into grade 1; ×/÷ one-digit fluency at ~9) as era gates — ceremonial and numeracy-chain-only, never cross-domain (§3). [R3 §2.1, §3.2, §6.1; CR14]
- **English literacy runs on a UK-style SSP sequence begun at 6–7 by our own ruling** (CR2 — UK Reception starts statutory SSP at 4–5 and decodes CVC words by 5, so this is a deliberately delayed start reaching the same endpoint by ~9, not "the UK clock"), and is checked against phonics-phase progress (GPC coverage, decodable-text reading), not school grades. Numeracy follows the international consensus clock (CR14's second scheduled run-ahead, per R3 §6), checked against the Estonian grade-3 list only as the ~9–10 legal checkpoint. [R3 §0.3, §5, §6; R2 §10.2; CR14]
- **Dose adequacy for the by-9 English endpoint (argued, not assumed).** Two phonics-led sessions/week (~20–25 explicit minutes, §3 template) is roughly a third of the UK classroom benchmark the endpoint claim cites (daily ~20-min whole-class SSP plus school-day reinforcement) — the claim survives because the comparison is not like-for-like: (a) **1:1 dosing *with delivery quality secured*** — every minute lands at the child's exact frontier with immediate feedback and zero waiting; but reconditioned in v1.6, because the tutoring literature this leans on (Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan 2020, ~0.35 SD, R2 §8) is dominated by *trained* tutors and paraprofessionals with protocols, and parent-delivered tutoring is that meta-analysis's weakest arm — so 1:1 counts as the argued advantage **only with the parent-delivery ruling below followed** (mini-guide + B22 audio models), never on parental goodwill alone; (b) **older-starter efficiency** — the start is deliberately delayed to 6–7, and later starters acquire GPCs measurably faster and catch up on equal endpoints in less instructional time (Suggate, in R3 §7's bibliography — the same evidence GP11 rests on); (c) the **oral-comprehension leg runs from 5** (GP10), so decoding instruction lands on prepared language rather than teaching sounds and meanings at once. **And the trajectory is monitored, not hoped for:** at each quarterly dim-check (§5.6), compare GPC-set coverage against the by-9 trajectory — roughly, single-letter GPCs + CVC blending secure by ~7; digraphs and common vowel teams by ~8; fluent decodable-text reading by ~9 — **and ask the rail question (v1.7, SGP S-8): is the English rail actually running at its CR18 floor?** — leg (c) of this argument is oral exposure, and its minutes are counted, not assumed. **If the child sits more than one phase behind, the lever sequence is ruled (v1.6): delivery first, dose second, professional eyes third.** (1) *Delivery check first:* the parent self-checks against the delivery mini-guide and the B22 audio models, and the quarterly listen includes **parent-transmitted GPC errors, not just coverage** — more dose of a flawed model makes the problem worse, not better. (2) *Then* add a third phonics-led session (swapped in for a booklet/free-choice slot) — **capped at one quarter consecutively**: §3's template runs the eight non-training-ground domains through the two booklet/free-choice sessions, so the swap squeezes them into one, a cost the framework now prices instead of ignoring (during a swap quarter, the surviving booklet session leans on B13's rotation for breadth, the warm-up-riding strands — letter formation, the handwriting passage, the typing opener — continue unharmed, and the strips carry the rest). (3) When a capped quarter has not closed the gap, the lever becomes the atypical-development triggers below — never more dose. [R2 §10.2; R3 §5, §7 (Suggate); CR2; CR3; CR14; GP10; GP11]
- **Parent SSP delivery (ruled, v1.6).** The **SSP Parent Delivery Mini-Guide** (~10 pages: pure phoneme articulation with no added schwa, the blending routine, the decodable-text routine, what a session looks like, the common errors) is a **named first-class deliverable of the booklet pipeline**, shipped before `lit-en-phonics-1` unlocks — the SSP schemes this framework cites all publish such guides, and §5.6 prices the parent's minutes while this ruling secures the parent's phonology. Every phonics booklet carries B22's per-GPC audio models and per-set pronunciation micro-guides; the quarterly trajectory check listens to the *child* for parent-transmitted errors. The by-9 endpoint argument above is conditioned on this ruling being followed, not on parental heroics. [R2 §8, §10.2; B22; CR2]
- **Atypical development (ruled, v1.6 — refer out, never diagnose).** The framework's individual-differences machinery is pace-shaped (mastery-not-age, placement, correctives, dose); a *specific learning difficulty* is not a pace problem, and the evidence reports never covered it — a silence inherited and now flagged. Named triggers → professional assessment (eripedagoog/logopeed via school or Rajaleidja): (i) phonemic-awareness sub-skills not consolidating by ~6.5 despite dose and format changes; (ii) persistent b/d-class reversals or letter–sound instability past ~7.5, especially with family history of reading difficulty; (iii) Archetype-B attention tallies unreachable across two consecutive quarters despite the GP17 ladder; (iv) the **bilingual masking asymmetry**, watched explicitly — Estonian's transparent orthography lets a dyslexic child decode passably while English exposes the difficulty (R2 §10.2 cuts both ways), so a persistent English-only lag beside clean Estonian is a trigger, not a comfort. With any documented difficulty: decay-dimming is **softened** (dims render as neutral refresh-queued marks, never grey-outs — the dimming poster is a demoralization engine for exactly the ADHD profile) and the dose levers defer to the professional's plan. [R2 §10.2; GP17; §5.1 Needs-polish]
- **Ahead of trajectory (ruled, v1.7 — the lag lever's mirror, SGP S-7).** §5.4 rules three steps for the child who falls behind and previously said nothing about the child who runs ahead. Ruled: **lead is normal and unalarming** — CR2's pull-not-push working as designed, and CR14's run-aheads make some lead structural. The response is **depth before acceleration** (GP17's ceiling response): stretch variants, B24's non-routine sets, two-representations demands — never simply surfacing the next rung faster, and never more volume. And the **school interface is named as a thing the family does on purpose**: the scheduled numeracy run-ahead makes a child fluent within 100 sitting through grade-1 counting lessons a *designed* collision, so the parent–teacher conversation about it — what the child can do, what depth or helper work school can offer, where the child simply practices patience gracefully — happens deliberately at the start of each school year, not after boredom curdles into a behavior report. [GP17; CR2; CR14; SGP S-6/S-7]

### 5.5 Motivation-safe mechanics (what we deliberately do NOT measure or reward)

No streaks; no points for time-on-task; no badges for attendance; no percentile/sibling comparison; no red ink; no timed-test aesthetics — and since v1.7 that includes **covert timing** (FIN-16): fluency is certified by the parent's **retrieval-listen** (prompt, strategy-free answers), never by a numeric latency bar or a stopwatch, visible or hidden — children detect covert evaluation faster than overt, and a parent glancing at a watch mid-check *is* the test aesthetic CR11 bans. Rewards mark new competence only. **The display law binds any surface the child sees, paper or screen** — the family progress app inherits it wholesale via CR19 (v1.7). [R4 A5, C4 anti-patterns; R2 r15, §9.4, §11 design implications; R1 BR12; CR19; FIN-16]

### 5.6 Operational feasibility — a week in the life, per band

The likeliest real-world failure mode of this system is parent overload, so the load is priced explicitly. The session (§3, CR16) is the **whole** daily system time; mechanics that would not fit inside it have been pruned by rule.

| | Child min/week | Parent min/week | School-side load (not ours, but on the same child — v1.7, FIN-8) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Band 1 (5–6)** | 6 × 10–15 ≈ 60–90 | ≈ same (it is co-play) + ~10 admin | Lasteaed day (play register; no homework) | Poster stamping + one evidence line on the rare mastery week |
| **Band 2 (6–7)** | 6 × 15–20 ≈ 90–120 | ~2/3 of sessions (retrieval strips and puzzles self-check, B15) + ~10 admin | Full koolieelik lasteaed day — itself delivering koolivalmidus prep (CR17) | An unlock trial some weeks (<10 min, inside the session) |
| **Band 3 (7–8, school in parallel)** | 5 × 15–20 ≈ 75–100 | ~40 (openers + checks only) + ~10 admin | School day + real homework from grade 1, est. ~15–30 min/day (Estonia's norm; not Finland's near-zero) | System yields to school homework — standing rule below, not just crunch weeks |
| **Band 4 (8–9)** | 5 × 20 ≈ 100 | ~30 + one ceremony/evidence entry | School day + homework, est. ~20–40 min/day | Child largely solo; parent verifies, celebrates |

**Plus, at every band, the family read-aloud rail (CR18): ~70 min/week of parent time** (~10 min/day, Estonian and English alternating or stacked, with the v1.7 English floor) — priced here honestly since v1.6 because it is the trunk's oral-leg delivery vehicle and was previously off the books; it is protected family ritual, not a schedulable slot, and a missed night is nothing (CR4). **And the weekly family meeting (CR20, v1.7): ~10 min/week of everyone's time** — jobs rotate, everyone reflects, the child chairs from ~7; priced here so the second and last standing exemption is on the books like the first.

**The whole child's day (ruled, v1.7, FIN-8 — §5.6 previously priced the system's minutes and never the child's day).** The school-side column above is the honest ledger of what else sits on the same child; nobody else was watching the sum. Two mechanisms follow: *(a)* a **combined daily desk cap at Bands 3–4** — system work + school homework together target **≤30–40 min/day**, and when the sum threatens the cap **the system auto-sheds first** (practice pages before strips — the minimum-viable-week ordering, applied daily); *(b)* the crunch-week yield is **promoted from exception to standing budget**: on *any* day school homework exceeds its estimate, the system session is the thing that shrinks, without declaration or make-up — "the system yields to school" is now a mechanism, not a sentence (the same promotion CR18 and the re-entry protocol performed for their values). [FIN-8; CR17; GP11]

**Pruned/batched by rule:** consolidation retests ride the B18 retrieval strips automatically (mechanism ruled in §5.1) — there are no separate retest sessions; **child-side node prescriptions are priced too, not just parent load** — every exercise cadence names its §3 template slot and at most two *slot-occupying* training-ground nodes sit in Practicing at once (strands riding warm-up pages or evidenced by school work don't count — §3 guard-rail (a)), so the per-node lines can never sum past the session row (§3 subordination rule); cold-start entry is one batched placement sweep (§5.1), never weeks of sequential unlock ceremony; decay timers are **not** tracked continuously — a once-per-quarter ~20-minute "dim check" sweep (at season or era boundaries) marks Needs-polish nodes, whose content then simply re-enters warm-up sampling; the evidence log is one line per keystone node, not per session; ceremonies happen at most monthly.

**The minimum-viable-week rule (named and promoted, v1.6 — so a tired parent can find it):** if a week does not fit, **drop practice pages, never the retrieval strips** (GP8) and keep the read-aloud rail if only one thing survives (CR18). The 6-session (Bands 1–2) / 5-session (Bands 3–4) figures are the *design* week; **a 4-session week — illness waves, travel, a normal two-job Estonian winter — is a normal week, not a failure week.** The whole edifice stays optional beside a functioning school (GP11).

**Re-entry protocol (ruled, v1.6 — the family's documented reality is interruption, so the mechanism matches the stated value):** CR4 already bans streaks *because* a family trip must never feel like losing; this is the machinery that makes it true. On a **declared family pause** (travel, illness): decay timers **suspend** for its duration and the poster never dims during one. After **any gap of ≥2 weeks**, declared or not: the first week back is **strips-plus-booklet only** — no dose sessions, no checks, no unlock trials; every training-ground node in Practicing **re-enters one rung down** (a different-representation rung, GP17's corrective idiom — never the page it left off on); and the consolidation-retest queue **resets to re-queue from the return date rather than accumulating** — a re-entry week greeted by a backlog of expired obligations is exactly the demoralization pattern §5.5 bans. [CR4; GP8; GP17; §5.1]

**Multi-child families (ruled, v1.6 — the project is named "kids-learning" and the docs designed for one child):** shared sessions are fine where frontiers allow (a booklet session can seat two children on their own pages); **one poster per child, always** — the poster is a personal assessment artifact and §5.5's sibling-comparison ban applies to wall displays too (two posters may hang in different rooms if comparison culture threatens); self-checking booklet pages are **consumables** — each child gets a fresh print (tile sheets and game boards are the reusable layer); a second child **enters via their own placement sweep** (§5.1), never via hand-me-down assumptions about what siblings of that age know; and siblings may star in each other's exercises (timing, audience, playtesting) exactly as the node cards already do — as helpers, never as benchmarks. [§5.1; §5.5; GP9]
