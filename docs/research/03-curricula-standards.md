# What National Curricula Expect of Children Ages 5–9: A Five-System Comparison

**Research report 03 — Curricula & standards**
**Scope:** Estonia (koolieelse lasteasutuse riiklik õppekava + põhikooli riikliku õppekava I kooliaste), Finland (ECEC / esiopetus / grades 1–2), England (EYFS + KS1/lower KS2), United States (Common Core K–3 + NGSS), Singapore (NEL Framework + Primary Mathematics syllabus).
**Purpose downstream:** (a) sequencing spine for the Civilization-style skill tree from age-5 foundations to career branches; (b) difficulty calibration for printable exercise booklets, starting with picture sudoku.
**Date:** 2026-08-30

---

## 0. Executive summary

1. **The five systems disagree loudly about *when* formal instruction starts (age 4–5 in England vs. 7 in Estonia/Finland/Singapore) but agree almost completely about *the order* in which skills develop.** The internal sequencing — oral language → phonological awareness → decoding → fluency → comprehension; counting → cardinality → part-whole → place value → operations → multiplicative reasoning — is essentially identical everywhere. This is excellent news for a skill tree: build the *order* from the consensus, and treat *age* as a tunable difficulty band, not a hard gate.
2. **The late-starting systems (Estonia, Finland, Singapore) outperform the early-starting ones (England, US) by ages 10 and 15** on PISA/TIMSS/PIRLS, which supports a skill tree that spends ages 5–6 on rich foundations (oral language, logic, patterns, subitising, motor, self-regulation) rather than accelerated formal drill. Estonia is Europe's top PISA performer; Singapore is the global one.
3. **A big hidden variable is orthography.** Estonian and Finnish are near-perfectly transparent (one letter ≈ one sound), so children reliably learn to decode within months of grade 1 at age 7 with a simple letter–sound–syllable method. English is the most opaque alphabetic orthography — decoding takes ~2.5× longer (Seymour, Aro & Erskine, 2003) and needs 2–3 years of *systematic synthetic phonics*. For an Estonian family learning in English, this means: **English literacy needs the English (UK/US) phonics timeline; Estonian literacy will largely take care of itself on the Estonian timeline. Do not assume one literacy track.**
4. **For numeracy, Singapore's Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract sequence + number bonds + bar models is the strongest sequencing model** and has already been imported wholesale by England ("maths mastery"). Use it as the numeracy spine, cross-checked against Estonian grade-level outcomes so the child is always at or ahead of school expectations.
5. **Picture sudoku sits at a real curricular node:** it trains one-to-one correspondence, systematic search, logical deduction (elimination), and working memory — pre-formal skills every framework names (Estonian *tunnetus- ja õpioskused*, Finnish transversal competence L1 "thinking and learning to learn", EYFS mathematics "pattern", Singapore's framework "processes + metacognition"). Calibration guidance in §6.

## 1. Grade mapping: who is where at each age

School-start policy is the single biggest structural difference. Ages below are typical (birthday effects shift ±1).

| Age | Estonia | Finland | England | US | Singapore |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | Lasteaed (kindergarten, middle years) | ECEC (päiväkoti) | **Reception** (formal EYFS year) → Year 1 | **Kindergarten** | K1 → K2 (preschool) |
| **6** | Lasteaed, final year (*koolieelik*, school-prep) | **Esiopetus** (compulsory pre-primary) | **Year 1** (full formal curriculum) | K → **Grade 1** | K2 |
| **7** | **Grade 1** (school entry) | **Grade 1** (school entry) | Year 2 | Grade 1 → 2 | **Primary 1** (school entry) |
| **8** | Grade 2 | Grade 2 | Year 3 | Grade 2 → 3 | Primary 2 |
| **9** | Grade 3 (end of I kooliaste) | Grade 3 | Year 4 | Grade 3 → 4 | Primary 3 |

Key consequence: **at age 6, an English child is being formally taught to read and write while an Estonian/Finnish child is in a play-based prep year** — yet by age 10 the Estonian/Finnish children read as well or better (PIRLS; Suggate et al., 2013 found no lasting advantage to earlier reading instruction once orthography is controlled).

## 2. Country profiles

### 2.1 Estonia

**Documents:**
- *Koolieelse lasteasutuse riiklik õppekava* (National Curriculum for Preschool Child Care Institutions, Government regulation, 2008) — [Riigi Teataja text](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/12970917).
- *Põhikooli riiklik õppekava* (National Curriculum for Basic Schools, 2011, substantially updated 2023, new versions in force from 2024) — [Riigi Teataja text](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/114012011001); subject syllabi at [oppekava.ee](https://oppekava.ee/matemaatika-ainekavad/).

**Kindergarten curriculum structure.** Seven learning-and-teaching domains: *mina ja keskkond* (me and the environment), *keel ja kõne* (language and speech), *eesti keel kui teine keel*, *matemaatika*, *kunst*, *muusika*, *liikumine* (movement) — plus four cross-cutting **general skills (üldoskused)**: play skills, cognitive and learning skills, social skills, self-management skills. Learning is explicitly through play and daily life; the curriculum states expected outcomes at ages 3, 5, and **6–7 (= school readiness, *koolivalmidus*)**.

**Expected outcomes of a 6–7-year-old (school-readiness profile, selected):**
- *Language:* pronounces all Estonian sounds; retells stories; **knows the letters; sounds out (veerib) 1–2-syllable words and recognises some familiar words in print; writes 1–2-syllable words in printed capitals**; knows some rhymes/poems by heart.
- *Mathematics:* **counts sets to 12 and knows the number line 1–12** (before/after a given number); recognises and begins writing numerals; **adds and subtracts within 5**; compares sets (more/fewer/equal); knows circle, square, triangle, rectangle; orders objects by size/length; knows days of the week and seasons; orients on a sheet of paper (top/bottom/left/right corner); measures with informal units; tells full hours on the clock.
- *Me and environment:* home, family, professions; Estonia and its symbols; traffic safety rules as a pedestrian; care for nature; basics of healthy behaviour.
- *Motor:* holds pencil correctly, cuts with scissors, throws/catches, basic gymnastics/skiing/skating exposure per local options.

**I kooliaste (grades 1–3, ages 7–10).** Subjects: eesti keel, matemaatika, loodusõpetus (nature study, from grade 1), inimeseõpetus (human/health studies), kunst, muusika, kehaline kasvatus (incl. **compulsory beginner swimming course**, target ability ~200 m), tööõpetus (craft), and A foreign language (usually English) starting **no later than grade 3**, with many schools starting earlier. The curriculum defines eight **general competences** including *digipädevus* (digital competence) and *õpipädevus* (learning to learn), which are cross-curricular rather than separate subjects; Estonia's **ProgeTiiger** programme has put Bee-Bots and simple robotics into kindergartens and grades 1–3 since 2012.

**End-of-grade-3 outcomes (selected):**
- *Estonian:* reads age-appropriate texts fluently and with comprehension; spells learned vocabulary substantially correctly in dictation; writes short connected texts; presents orally.
- *Mathematics:* numbers **0–10,000** (reads, writes, orders, written column +/−); **mental arithmetic within 100**; **full multiplication table fluent, with corresponding division**; two-step expressions with brackets; simple fractions **1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5**; units (cm/m/km, g/kg, s/min/h, €); one- and two-step word problems; point/segment/triangle/quadrilateral/circle, perimeter of a polygon; recognises cube, sphere, cylinder. (Confirmed against current ainekava summaries, e.g. [Westholm grade 1–3 syllabus](https://westholm.ee/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Matemaatika-ainekava-1.-3.-klass.pdf).)
- *Loodusõpetus:* observation and simple measurement as method; living vs non-living; plants, animals, human senses and body; seasons and weather observation; organisms and their habitats; simple plans/maps; Estonian nature.
- *Inimeseõpetus:* emotions and their naming, friendship and conflict resolution, family, daily rhythm and time use, health, safety, money basics.

### 2.2 Finland

**Documents:** *Varhaiskasvatussuunnitelman perusteet* (National Core Curriculum for ECEC, 2018/2022); *Esiopetuksen opetussuunnitelman perusteet 2014* (pre-primary, compulsory since 2015, age 6); *Perusopetuksen opetussuunnitelman perusteet 2014* (basic education; grades 1–2 defined as one unit). Published by the Finnish National Agency for Education (Opetushallitus).

**Philosophy.** The most deliberately non-academic early system of the five. Esiopetus (age 6) is organised around five *entities* — "rich world of language", "diverse forms of expression", "me and our community", "exploring my environment", "growing and developing" — with **no reading or arithmetic outcome requirements**. What *is* required: phonological play, interest in text, classification/seriation/patterning, self-care skills, group skills. The 2014 basic-education curriculum defines seven **transversal competences (L1–L7)** — thinking & learning to learn; cultural competence; taking care of oneself; **multiliteracy (L4)**; **ICT competence (L5)**; working-life competence; participation — threaded through every subject, plus at least one multidisciplinary "phenomenon" module per year.

**Grades 1–2 (ages 7–9):**
- *Literacy:* the **KÄTS method** (kirjain–äänne–tavu–sana: letter–sound–syllable–word). Because Finnish orthography is fully transparent, most children decode within the first months of grade 1 and read functionally by its end; grade 2 is fluency and comprehension. Heikki Lyytinen's Jyväskylä group built **GraphoGame** on this letter–sound learning model.
- *Numeracy:* grade 1 numbers 0–100 with strong emphasis on part-whole understanding and +/− within 20; grade 2 range to 1000, place value, multiplication concept and first tables; **programming thinking is written into the math syllabus from grade 1** ("giving and testing step-by-step instructions").
- *Ympäristöoppi* (environmental studies) from grade 1 fuses biology, geography, physics, chemistry and health education around inquiry.
- Assessment is verbal/formative; no standardised testing in these years.

**Key interpreters:** Pasi Sahlberg (*Finnish Lessons*, 2011) on the system logic; Lyytinen on early reading; the national "Let's Read Together" family literacy programmes.

### 2.3 England (EYFS + KS1, lower KS2)

**Documents:** *Statutory framework for the Early Years Foundation Stage* (DfE, revised 2021, minor updates 2023–24); non-statutory guidance *Development Matters* (2021) and *Birth to 5 Matters* (2021); *National Curriculum in England* (2014) for KS1 (Years 1–2) and KS2; *The Reading Framework* (DfE 2021/2023).

**EYFS (to age 5, end of Reception).** Seven areas: three **prime** (communication & language; physical development; personal, social & emotional development) and four **specific** (literacy; mathematics; understanding the world; expressive arts & design). Assessed against 17 **Early Learning Goals**, notably:
- *Literacy ELGs:* say a sound for each letter and ≥10 digraphs; **read words consistent with phonic knowledge and simple sentences**; **write recognisable letters and simple phrases** spelled phonetically.
- *Mathematics ELGs:* **deep understanding of numbers to 10; subitise to 5; recall number bonds to 5 (incl. subtraction facts) and some to 10**; compare quantities; verbal counting beyond 20; explore patterns. (Note: deliberately *depth to 10*, not range — a 2021 shift toward mastery thinking.)
- *PSED ELGs:* self-regulation (follow multi-step instructions, manage impulses), managing self (independence, hygiene, rules), building relationships.
- *Physical development ELGs:* gross motor (balance, negotiate space) and **fine motor: hold a pencil effectively — tripod grip — use scissors and cutlery, begin accurate letter formation**.

**KS1 and lower KS2:**
- *Literacy:* **systematic synthetic phonics is statutory** (Rose Review, 2006; validated schemes like Little Wandle, Read Write Inc). **Year 1 Phonics Screening Check** (age 6): 40 words including pseudowords. Year 2: fluency ~90 wpm, common exception words, suffix spelling; Years 3–4: statutory spelling lists, joined handwriting, paragraphing.
- *Maths:* Y1 count to/across 100, **number bonds to 20**, +/− within 20; Y2 place value to 100, **×/÷ with 2, 5, 10 tables**, fractions 1/2, 1/4, 1/3, 3/4; Y3 3-digit numbers, tables 3, 4, 8, formal columns; Y4 **all tables to 12×12** tested in the statutory Multiplication Tables Check (age 8–9). England has explicitly imported Singapore/Shanghai "**maths mastery**" via the NCETM Maths Hubs.
- *Science:* statutory weekly from Y1 (plants; animals incl. humans; everyday materials; seasonal change → Y2 habitats, uses of materials → Y3 rocks, light, forces & magnets, plants, skeletons) with "working scientifically" strands.
- *Computing:* **statutory from Year 1**: understand what algorithms are; **create and debug simple programs**; use logical reasoning to predict program behaviour; e-safety.
- *Research anchors:* Sylva et al., **EPPE/EPPSE** longitudinal study (quality of early provision predicts outcomes); Rose (2006); DfE Reading Framework.

### 2.4 United States (Common Core K–3 + NGSS)

**Documents:** *Common Core State Standards* for ELA and Mathematics (NGA/CCSSO, 2010; adopted, sometimes renamed, by 40+ states); *Next Generation Science Standards* (2013); CASEL framework for SEL (state-by-state); CSTA K–12 CS standards (non-mandatory).

**Mathematics staircase (the most precisely specified of the five):**
- **K (age 5–6):** count to 100 by ones and tens (K.CC.1); count objects with cardinality; compare sets; **fluently add/subtract within 5** (K.OA.5); decompose numbers ≤10 into pairs; understand 11–19 as ten + ones; name shapes incl. 3-D.
- **G1 (6–7):** add/subtract **within 20** (fluent within 10); unknown-addend problems as subtraction; place value to 120; measure lengths with non-standard units; time to hour/half-hour; halves and quarters of shapes.
- **G2 (7–8):** **fluent +/− within 100** (mental within 20 from memory); place value to 1000; skip-count by 5s/10s/100s; foundations of multiplication via arrays and equal groups (no fluency demand); standard length units (cm/in); money word problems; time to 5 minutes; picture/bar graphs.
- **G3 (8–9):** **multiply/divide within 100, products of two one-digit numbers known from memory by year-end**; two-step word problems; **fractions as numbers on the number line** (3.NF — a celebrated design choice); area as multiplication, perimeter; time to the minute; scaled graphs.
- *Design principles worth stealing:* narrow-but-deep "focus" per grade; "coherence" links (each year's work explicitly builds on named prior standards); fluency expectations separated from conceptual ones.
- **ELA:** Reading Foundational Skills ladder (print concepts → phonological awareness → phonics → fluency) K–3; the well-known **"learning to read → reading to learn" transition is placed at grade 3**; writing in three genres (opinion/informative/narrative) from K. The post-2019 **"Science of Reading" movement** (state phonics mandates; Mississippi's NAEP gains) pushed practice toward the UK's systematic-phonics position.
- **NGSS:** K pushes/pulls, needs of living things, weather; G1 light & sound, structure/function in plants & animals, sun/moon patterns; G2 properties of matter, biodiversity/habitat, Earth's changing surface; G3 forces & motion, life cycles, inheritance & traits, ecosystems & climate. Format: performance expectations combining practice + core idea + crosscutting concept.

### 2.5 Singapore

**Documents:** *Nurturing Early Learners (NEL) Framework* (MOE, revised 2022) for ages 4–6; *Mathematics Syllabus Primary One to Six* (MOE, 2021 implementation); *English Language Syllabus* with the **STELLAR** programme; *Science Syllabus Primary* (science begins only at **P3**).

**Preschool (K1–K2, ages 4–6).** NEL learning areas: aesthetics & creative expression; **discovery of the world**; language & literacy; **motor skills development**; **numeracy**; social & emotional development. K2 numeracy expectations are modest on paper (rote count to 20+, count sets to 10, simple patterns, shapes, comparison words) — the famous rigor starts at P1. (In practice much of the cohort enters P1 already reading and counting well beyond this, via home/enrichment — treat published K2 expectations as a floor.)

**Primary Mathematics (the world's most-copied sequence):**
- *Framework:* a pentagon with **mathematical problem solving** at the centre and five faces — concepts, skills, **processes, attitudes, metacognition**. Pedagogy: **CPA (Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract)**, straight from Bruner's enactive–iconic–symbolic; **number bonds** (part-whole diagrams) from P1; the **model method / bar modelling** (developed under Kho Tek Hong at MOE's CDD in the early 1980s; codified in *The Singapore Model Method for Learning Mathematics*, MOE 2009) formally from P2–P3; teaching by systematic **variation** (Dienes); fewer topics, taught to mastery.
- **P1 (age 7):** numbers to 100; +/− within 100; **multiplication and division as concepts** (equal groups/sharing, within 40/20 — no table memorisation); money; length with non-standard→standard units; time to hour/half-hour; basic shapes; picture graphs.
- **P2 (8):** numbers to 1000; +/− with regrouping; **tables of 2, 3, 4, 5, 10**; fractions of a whole, add/subtract like fractions within 1; length/mass/volume in standard units; time to 5 min; **bar models introduced** for 1–2-step word problems.
- **P3 (9):** numbers to 10,000; **tables of 6, 7, 8, 9; division with remainder**; equivalent fractions, comparing/ordering; add/subtract related fractions; money to $; time to the minute + duration; area & perimeter; angles (right angle as benchmark); bar graphs with scales; bar modelling standard for two-step problems.
- *Science:* deliberately **absent until P3**, then launched with an inquiry syllabus around five themes (Diversity, Cycles, Systems, Interactions, Energy) — later than every other system, but deeper immediately.
- *Assessment culture:* since 2019 **no exams in P1–P2** (holistic development push); high-stakes testing arrives with PSLE at 12.
- *Interpreters:* Kho Tek Hong; Bruner (1966); Richard Skemp (relational vs instrumental understanding, 1976); Liping Ma (*Knowing and Teaching Elementary Mathematics*, 1999) for the "profound understanding of fundamental mathematics" ideal Singapore operationalises.

Research anchor: Durlak et al. (2011) meta-analysis — structured SEL programmes add ~11 percentile points to academic achievement; Blair & Razza (2007) and Adele Diamond's work show **self-regulation/executive function at 5–6 predicts academics better than IQ or entry-level reading/math**. This justifies making self-regulation an explicit early trunk of the skill tree, not decoration.

### 3.5 Motor (gross + fine)

| Age | Consensus milestone | Estonia | Finland | England | US | Singapore |

## 4. Consensus sequencing: the invariant chains

These orderings hold in *all five* systems and in the research base; they are the safe dependency edges for the skill tree.

**Literacy chain**
1. Oral language & vocabulary (listening comprehension leads reading comprehension by years — Scarborough's Reading Rope, 2001)
2. Phonological awareness: word → syllable → rhyme/onset → **phoneme** (blending & segmenting)
3. Letter knowledge (shapes + sounds; names secondary)
4. Decoding (letter-sound assembly; in English: systematic GPC sequence, decodable text)
5. Orthographic mapping → sight vocabulary → **fluency** (Ehri's phases)
6. Comprehension strategies & text structures; spelling conventions lag reading by ~1 year
7. Composition (transcription must be near-automatic first — handwriting + spelling free the working memory; Berninger's "simple view of writing")

**Numeracy chain**
1. Sorting/classification, patterning, seriation (pre-number logic — Piaget, Clements & Sarama)
2. Counting principles: one-to-one, stable order, **cardinality**, abstraction, order-irrelevance (Gelman & Gallistel, 1978)
3. Subitising (perceptual → conceptual) and comparison
4. **Part-whole understanding / number bonds** (the hinge concept — UK ELGs, Singapore P1, Finnish gr 1 all pivot here)
5. Addition/subtraction as part-whole stories → fact fluency within 10 → within 20
6. Place value (unitising ten) → multi-digit +/−
7. Multiplicative thinking: equal groups → arrays → skip counting → tables → division (inverse) → remainders
8. Fractions: fair sharing → part of whole → **number on the number line** (US G3 formulation is the best-designed) → equivalence
9. Throughout, parallel strands: measurement (non-standard → standard units), geometry (recognise → describe → compose), data (sort → pictograph → scaled bar graph)

**Science chain:** observe & describe → sort/classify → compare & measure → predict-test-record (fair test) → explain with evidence → simple models. Content (plants/animals/materials/Earth/space) cycles through this method ladder.

**SEL chain:** co-regulation → self-regulation → emotion vocabulary → perspective-taking → conflict resolution → collaboration roles → self-evaluation & goal-setting. (CASEL's five competences, ordered developmentally.)

**Motor chain:** gross before fine; proximal before distal (shoulder→wrist→fingers); tripod grip → shape copying → letter formation → automatic handwriting → speed/stamina; scissors/threading/construction run parallel as strength-and-precision builders.

**Digital chain:** unplugged sequencing → executing/giving instructions → arrow programming → block sequences → loops/events → variables; e-safety and media judgment threaded from first contact.

## 5. Notable differences (and what the evidence says)

| Dimension | The spread | Evidence & implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Formal start age** | England 4–5 → US 5–6 → EE/FI/SG 7 | Late starters lead PISA/TIMSS at 15. Suggate, Schaughency & Reese (2013): reading age-of-onset advantage washes out by ~11. Sahlberg, Whitebread, and the EPPE study all point to *quality of early interaction*, not early formal drill. **Skill tree: keep ages 5–6 rich but playful; formal-looking worksheets earn their place only where the child pulls.** |
| **Phonics intensity** | England statutory SSP + national check; US converging via "Science of Reading"; EE/FI barely need it (transparent orthography); SG systematic via STELLAR | Seymour, Aro & Erskine (2003): English children take ~2.5× longer to reach accurate decoding than Finnish. Ziegler & Goswami's grain-size theory explains why. **For this family: run an explicit UK-style phonics sequence for English; let Estonian reading follow the light KÄTS-style letter–sound–syllable path.** Castles, Rastle & Nation (2018) is the definitive modern synthesis. |
| **Counting range vs depth at 5–6** | US K counts to 100 (range); UK Reception caps at 10 (depth: subitising + bonds); EE koolieelik: 12 | The UK's 2021 "depth over range" revision reflects mastery research (and Singapore practice). **Booklets: prioritise subitising, part-whole and comparison to 10 over big-number rote counting.** |
| **Multiplication timing** | England Y2 (age 6–7, tables 2/5/10) earliest; SG P1 concepts-only; EE/FI/US ~8–9 for fluency | All converge on fluency by 9. Concept-before-drill (SG) is the defensible order. |
| **Science start** | England Y1 (age 5) earliest; SG P3 (age 9) latest — yet SG tops TIMSS science at 10 | Inquiry habits can be built content-light; formal content can compress. **Tree: thin observation trunk from 5, content bursts from 7.** |
| **Digital start** | EE kindergarten (ProgeTiiger) & England Y1 statutory earliest; FI embedded-in-math; SG latest | No harm signal for early *unplugged/robotic* computing; strong national bet by both tech-heavy small states (EE, UK). |
| **Handwriting** | EE teaches joined *kirjatähed* gr 1; FI dropped mandatory cursive (2016) for print+typing; US cursive optional; UK joins at Y2–3 | James & Engelhardt (2012): forming letters by hand aids letter learning; the *joined vs print* question is cultural, the *by hand* part is not. |
| **Assessment culture** | England heaviest (RBA, phonics check Y1, MTC Y4); US state tests from G3; EE/FI descriptive only; SG abolished P1–P2 exams (2019) | The top performers test least at 5–9. **The skill tree's "XP" should come from mastery demonstrations, not test mimicry.** |
| **SEL placement** | EE uniquely has a *subject* (inimeseõpetus gr 1–3); UK makes PSED a prime area at 5; FI/SG embed; US fragmented | Durlak (2011), Diamond (2013): explicit is better than incidental. Estonia's subject-based model is a good template for a visible SEL branch. |
| **Craft/practical work** | EE tööõpetus and FI käsityö are real subjects with real tools; absent elsewhere | Unique Nordic/Baltic asset; feeds motor + planning + persistence. Worth a small "maker" branch. |

## 6. Implications for our deliverables

### 6.1 For the skill tree (Civ-style)

- **Six branches match the six domains** (literacy splits into Estonian-track and English-track sub-branches that share phonological-awareness ancestors but diverge at decoding).
- **Anchor the timeline to the Estonian grade calendar** (family reality: lasteaed → koolieelik at 6 → kool at 7), but let the English-literacy and numeracy branches run ahead on the UK/Singapore schedules, since the school system will not supply English phonics or CPA math early.
- **Recommended hard gates (consensus-safe dependency edges):**
  - counting-with-cardinality → number bonds to 5 → bonds to 10 → +/− within 20 → place value → ×-as-groups → tables → division → fractions-on-a-line;
  - phoneme blending → CVC decoding → digraphs → fluency → comprehension-strategy nodes; oral-vocabulary nodes feed comprehension directly (two-strand Reading Rope shape);
  - tripod grip → shape copying → letter formation → writing stamina (gate *composition* nodes on transcription, not on ideas);
  - unplugged sequencing → Bee-Bot/arrows → ScratchJr → Scratch (this is the software-engineer branch's on-ramp);
  - self-regulation node at tier 1 as a prerequisite feeding *every* branch (the research licence for this is Blair & Razza / Diamond).
- **Two "boss milestones" every system agrees on:** fluent +/− within 20 at ~7, one-digit ×/÷ fluency at ~9. Use them as epoch boundaries in the tree (like Civ era transitions), with EE grade-3 outcomes (§2.1) as the age-9 checkpoint list — it is compact, official, and the family's legal reference point.
- Career branches (doctor, software engineer, manager…) should *not* have age-5-9-specific content beyond the on-ramps above; what they need from this band is the shared trunk plus: observation/inquiry habits (doctor), computational chain (software engineer), SEL/collaboration/goal-setting chain (manager).

### 6.2 For the booklets (starting with picture sudoku)

- **Picture sudoku placement:** pre-number logic tier (age 5 entry), inside the numeracy branch's "patterns & logic" node cluster; it exercises one-to-one correspondence, systematic search, elimination, and working memory — named in Estonian üldoskused (tunnetus- ja õpioskused), Finnish L1, EYFS pattern strand, and Singapore's "processes/metacognition" framework faces.
- **Difficulty ladder (evidence-aligned):** 4×4 pictures with 6–8 givens (age 4–5) → 4×4 fewer givens (5) → 6×6 pictures (6, matches working-memory growth) → 4×4 with *numerals* as a deliberate picture→symbol bridge (6, mirrors CPA's pictorial→abstract move) → 6×6 numerals (7) → 9×9 with sub-boxes only at 8–9. Introduce each new size with a solved example and one "find the mistake" puzzle (debugging — doubles as computational thinking).
- **Booklet pipeline by age band, aligned to the matrix:** tracing/pre-writing + subitising dot cards (5); number-bond part-whole diagrams and CVC phonics for the English track (6); +/− within 20 with bar-model pictures (7); tables via arrays and skip-counting tracks (8); fraction number lines and two-step word problems (9). Every math booklet should follow **CPA within the page**: picture problem → same problem as diagram → same as bare number sentence.
- **Print advantage:** handwriting/fine-motor research (§3.5) means paper booklets are not a compromise but the *correct medium* for ages 5–7 transcription and early math; save screens for the digital branch proper.

## 7. Key researchers and works (short annotated list)

**Start age, play, systems:** Pasi Sahlberg (2011) *Finnish Lessons*; Suggate, Schaughency & Reese (2013) — later reading starters catch up; Sylva et al. — EPPE/EPPSE longitudinal study; David Whitebread (Cambridge PEDAL) — play and self-regulation; Hirsh-Pasek & Golinkoff — guided play; James Heckman — early-investment economics.

**Executive function & SEL:** Adele Diamond (2013) "Executive Functions" (*Annual Review of Psychology*); Blair & Razza (2007); Durlak et al. (2011) SEL meta-analysis; CASEL framework.

**Motor:** Grissmer et al. (2010) — fine motor as predictor; James & Engelhardt (2012) — handwriting and the reading brain.
